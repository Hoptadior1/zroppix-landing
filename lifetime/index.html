WHY CHECK AT ALL

Is this really a deal? Check before you pay.

A discount tag only compares the price to another price. These checks show why it’s worth a quick look. They don’t prove every sale is fake.

About 6 in 10

In a 2017 check of 1,000 Amazon products, about 6 in 10 crossed-out “was” prices were higher than anything Amazon had charged in the previous 3 months.

Counts only products that showed a crossed-out price. A 2017 survey by a consumer group, so things may have changed.
60¢ per $100

A reporter checked about 50 things he’d bought in the past six months against their prices on day two of Amazon’s October 2025 sale. Waiting for the sale would have saved him about 60 cents on every $100.

Just 50 items he chose, and one sale. Amazon disagrees with how the test was framed.
$100, not $700

One shopper took Amazon to court, saying a TV marked “40% off” had sold for only about $100 less shortly before the sale. The court dismissed the case in 2024 without deciding whether the prices were misleading.

This is his claim, not a finding.

Sources: our write-up of the Consumer Watchdog survey and the Washington Post test, and our summary of the lawsuits. Each links to the original sources.
