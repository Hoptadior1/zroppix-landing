How to read the prices on this page

Cards with a price line show a report from a named source on a specific date. That is different from a live price, and it's why the source sits under each one. Cards without a price line link to the product's price page and to Amazon, where you can see today's price.

  • Reported means a deal site or publication posted that price on that date. Many such deals have expired.
  • List or regular price is the seller's reference price. A badge on Amazon compares against a reference price too, so check what it's compared with.
  • We are rebuilding the remaining price pages in batches to the same standard, so more cards will gain a dated price over time.

Questions

Do the prices on this page update live?

No. Every price is a dated report from a named source, shown with its date. They are reference points, not today's prices. Open the Amazon listing, or use the Zroppix extension, to see the live price and its history.

How do you pick the products?

We list products we have researched and sourced ourselves. Each links to a full price page with the dated prices, the source for each, what to check before buying and an honest note on what we couldn't confirm.

Do you earn from these links?

Yes. As an Amazon Associate, Zroppix earns from qualifying purchases. It costs you nothing extra. Zroppix is also our own product, and we earn when you buy it.

Is a reported sale price still available?

Often not. Deals are posted on a date and many expire. We label every price with its date for that reason, and tell you when a deal is marked expired.

Is the Zroppix extension free?

Yes. The extension is free to install and shows 90 days of price history on Amazon listings with a BUY or WAIT verdict. The founder deal is an optional one-time $149 for lifetime access, limited to 497 people ever.

What if the product I want isn't listed?

Type its name in the search box above. If we don't have a page for it, the button searches Amazon for you in a new tab. Zroppix still shows you the price history when you open the listing.

Can I trust a percentage-off badge?

Not on its own. A badge compares today's price with a reference price the seller chooses. A price history shows what the listing actually sold for, which is why we show dated prices instead.
