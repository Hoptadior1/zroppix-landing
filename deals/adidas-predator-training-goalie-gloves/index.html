Training gloves vs match gloves

Brands sell a ladder of gloves from basic to elite. The key trade-off is grip versus lifespan:

  • Premium match gloves use very soft, tacky latex that grips brilliantly but wears out quickly. Keepers who use them often keep a second pair for training.
  • Training gloves use tougher latex built to survive repeated sessions. They grip well enough for most levels but are not quite as tacky.

If you play or practise several times a week, a training-level glove is the better value: you spend less per session and you are not afraid to dive on a hard pitch. Save premium gloves for the games that matter, if you buy them at all.

How to choose the right size

Fit is the number one reason gloves disappoint, and brands do not size identically. Measure first:

  1. Measure around the widest part of your palm, not including the thumb.
  2. Measure from the crease of your wrist to the tip of your middle finger.
  3. Compare both with adidas's own size chart and not another brand's.

Fingertips should almost reach the end of the glove, with room to flex but no bunching. If the latex folds over at the fingertips, the glove is too big. If your fingers are pushed against the end, it is too small. When you fall between sizes, many keepers choose the larger size for comfort, because gloves tighten slightly when wet.

Kids and growthYoung keepers grow fast. Buy a size that fits now. Gloves that are too large slip and hurt wrists and fingers when diving.

Glove cuts: what they mean

The "cut" is how the fingers are built inside the glove. It changes the fit and feel, so it is worth knowing the basics:

  • Flat palm: a simple, roomier construction. Often easier on a budget and comfortable for beginners.
  • Roll finger: latex wraps around the fingers for more surface contact and a fuller grip.
  • Negative cut: stitching sits inside the fingers for a snug, close fit.
  • Hybrid: mixes styles for balance.

Cuts vary between models, so check the product description and images on the listing to see which one the Predator Training uses. If you are unsure, flat and roll constructions are the easiest to live with.

How to make goalkeeper gloves last

Most gloves die from poor care, not from saves. Treat them like this:

  • Dampen the palms with a little water before play to improve grip. Do not soak them.
  • Rinse after use with cool water to remove mud and sweat. Do not wring or twist the latex.
  • Dry them flat or hung up in the shade. Heat from radiators, car heaters or direct sun breaks down the latex.
  • Do not machine wash or tumble dry.
  • Store them flat or open, not balled up in a bag. Some keepers put the pair palm-to-palm to keep them clean.
  • Use them on the right surface. Rough artificial turf and concrete scrape latex away quickly.

Frequently asked questions

What size goalkeeper gloves should I buy?

Measure your palm circumference and wrist-to-middle-finger length and compare with adidas's size chart. If you are between sizes, many keepers choose the larger one for comfort.

Are training gloves good enough for matches?

For most casual and amateur matches, yes. Premium match gloves offer tackier latex but wear out faster.

How do I clean goalkeeper gloves?

Rinse with cool water, avoid wringing, and dry them in the shade. Do not use a washing machine or direct heat.

Should I wet goalkeeper gloves before playing?

Lightly dampening the palms improves grip for most latex gloves. Do not soak them.

How long do goalkeeper gloves last?

It depends on how often and where you play. Regular use on rough surfaces can wear a pair out in weeks, while careful use on grass lasts much longer.

What cut is best for beginners?

Flat or roll constructions are generally comfortable and easy to live with. Check the listing for the cut used on this model.

Do gloves fit differently by brand?

Yes. Always use the brand's own size chart.
