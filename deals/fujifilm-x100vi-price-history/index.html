Fujifilm X100VI Price History — What's Actually Happening

The Fujifilm X100VI launched in February 2024 at $1,599. That's still the official MSRP today. But the camera went viral almost immediately — a wave of travel and street photographers posting unedited JPEGs using Fujifilm's film simulation color science — and demand has outstripped supply ever since. The practical result: real-world prices have run hundreds of dollars above MSRP for most of the camera's life, not below it.

Confirmed data points from mid-2026: Amazon's own direct stock was listed as out of stock as of June 2026 (CamelCamelCamel), with third-party sellers on Amazon asking $2,399. A separate camera price tracker showed $2,238 in May 2026, and another confirmed $2,299 in a listing checked June 21, 2026. Even used X100V (the previous generation) units are selling for $1,518+ — often close to or above current X100VI MSRP.

Why the Shortage Persists

Multiple 2026 sources point to the same underlying cause: production hasn't kept pace with sustained demand since the viral launch. One reviewer's 2026 buyer's guide notes that current MSRP-adjacent pricing is elevated compared to the original launch price, attributing part of the gap to recent tariffs affecting US camera pricing broadly. Separately, at least one long-term reviewer reports Fujifilm shifted some production to China during 2026 specifically to increase supply, with early signs of the situation improving — though demand has continued to outstrip that improved supply as of mid-2026.

💡A 2026 alternative worth knowing aboutThe Ricoh GR IV, released more recently, has emerged as a popular alternative compact for buyers priced out of or unable to find X100VI stock. Reviewers who've compared both describe them as excellent but offering meaningfully different shooting experiences — worth researching separately if X100VI availability or pricing is a dealbreaker for you.

What Is the Fujifilm X100VI

The Fujifilm X100VI is a fixed-lens compact camera built around a 40MP X-Trans BSI-CMOS 5 HR sensor. The fixed lens is a 23mm f/2 Fujinon — equivalent to 35mm full-frame — optically recomputed for the 40MP sensor's resolution. It's paired, for the first time in the X100 series, with in-body image stabilization (IBIS) rated at 6 stops of compensation.

What differentiates the X100VI from other cameras at this price point is its hybrid optical/electronic viewfinder — toggling between a bright, lag-free optical viewfinder and an electronic viewfinder showing exposure preview and focus peaking. The film simulation engine — Fujifilm's proprietary color science — produces JPEG files many photographers find require no editing, which was the central reason this camera went viral in the first place.

Is the X100VI Worth the Premium?

At MSRP ($1,599), the X100VI is excellent value — the 40MP sensor, IBIS, hybrid viewfinder, and film simulations in a pocketable body are genuinely hard to match. At the $2,200-2,400 street prices commonly seen through 2026, the calculus changes: you're paying $600-800 for immediate availability rather than for the camera's specs alone. Whether that premium is worth it depends entirely on how much you value having it now versus waiting for supply to normalize further — reviewers who've tracked the shortage into 2026 report gradual improvement, so patience may be rewarded, though there's no guaranteed timeline.

Fujifilm X100VI vs X100V — Should You Consider the Older Model

The Fujifilm X100V remains a legitimately excellent camera, but the same supply pressure affecting the X100VI has pushed used X100V prices up too — sometimes close to or above current X100VI MSRP, per multiple 2026 buyer's guides. This means the X100V isn't a reliable "cheaper alternative" the way it might be for a normally-supplied product line; check current used pricing carefully rather than assuming it's automatically the budget option.
