WHOOP 5 — How the Subscription Model Works

WHOOP uses a subscription model that differs from most fitness trackers — there's no separate upfront hardware cost. The WHOOP 5 sensor comes bundled with an active membership. Three tiers launched alongside WHOOP 5 in May 2025: One at $199/year (hardware plus core app access), Peak at $239/year (adds a wireless charging accessory plus coaching features), and Life at $359/year (adds advanced health monitoring). If you cancel, you return the hardware.

Rather than guess at a future discount, here's what actually happened: the confirmed Prime Day 2026 promotion discounted the Peak tier by 21%, from $239 to $189 — briefly making Peak cheaper than the standard One tier price. That's a real, independently confirmed data point, and it's a better reference than an unverified "all-time low" figure.

WHOOP 5 vs WHOOP 4.0

WHOOP 5 launched May 2025 with reported upgrades over the WHOOP 4.0 including roughly 14-day battery life, a smaller sensor, and a new Healthspan tracking feature. WHOOP 4.0 previously used a single flat membership price rather than tiers; the tiered structure (One/Peak/Life) is new as of the WHOOP 5 launch.

Who WHOOP 5 Is Actually For

WHOOP is built for athletes and serious fitness users focused on training load and recovery — it has no screen, no GPS, and no smartwatch functions. It's purely a physiological sensor (HRV, heart rate, sleep staging, strain) that feeds into daily recovery guidance through the app. If you want GPS for runs, notifications on your wrist, or general wellness tracking in a more discreet form factor, a Garmin watch or Oura Ring may suit you better.

✅

WHOOP offers a free trial period for new subscribers before you commit to a paid tier — a reasonable way to test fit before paying for a full year, regardless of what promotion is currently running.
