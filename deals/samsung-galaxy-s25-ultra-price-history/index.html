What the Galaxy S25 Ultra is, and where it sits now

The Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra is Samsung's top-tier phone of the 2025 generation. It launched in January 2025 at a US list price of $1,299, has a large display, a 200MP main camera, a built-in S Pen and a 5,000mAh battery, and is powered by a Snapdragon 8 Elite chip. Samsung has since released the Galaxy S26 Ultra, so the S25 Ultra is now the previous generation.

That matters for price, because previous-generation phones are often marked down once a newer one is out. Whether the S25 Ultra's price is actually low today depends on its recent history, which we can't verify here, so the sections below show how to check it.

Is it still a good buy?

It can suit you if you want a flagship Android phone with a built-in stylus, strong cameras and a big display, and you don't need the newest generation. Samsung advertises long software support for the S25 series, so check Samsung's page for the current commitment.

Consider the S26 Ultra if the price gap is small, since a newer phone gets updates for longer and has newer hardware. Consider other brands if you prefer iOS or want a smaller phone.

The S25 Ultra doesn't include a charger in the box, which is Samsung's long-standing practice. Check the listing, and budget for a charger if you don't have a compatible one.

Pick the right version first

  • Storage: 256GB, 512GB and 1TB options exist. Storage can't be expanded, so choose with your photo and video habits in mind.
  • Unlocked or carrier: carrier-locked phones are often cheaper because of plan conditions. An unlocked phone works on any compatible network.
  • New or renewed: a renewed or open-box phone can be cheaper, but check the grade, the battery condition and the return window. Read our Warehouse, Outlet and Renewed guide first.
  • Seller: Amazon or Samsung is safest on a phone this expensive.

Common mistakes when buying a flagship phone

  • Buying a carrier-locked phone by accident. A low price may come with plan conditions.
  • Paying the premium for storage you won't use.
  • Ignoring the newer model. If the gap is small, the newer phone may be the better deal.
  • Trusting a "percent off" badge. Compare against recorded price history.
  • Forgetting the case and charger. They add to the real cost.
