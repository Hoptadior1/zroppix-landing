Traeger Pro 575 — What Makes It Different From Charcoal and Gas Grills

The Pro 575 burns compressed wood pellets in flavors like hickory, apple, mesquite, and cherry. The D2 direct drivetrain feeds pellets from an 18-pound hopper into the fire pot automatically, maintaining your set temperature within ±15°F throughout the cook. You set the temperature, load the food, and walk away — the grill manages fuel and temperature automatically. This is fundamentally different from charcoal or gas grilling where you are actively managing the fire. The WiFi connectivity via the Traeger app means you can monitor the grill temperature and internal food temperature from your phone and receive notifications when your food reaches target temperature.

Traeger Pro 575 — 6-in-1 Versatility Explained

The 6-in-1 designation means the Pro 575 functions as a grill, smoker, baker, roaster, braiser, and BBQ unit. At 450°F maximum temperature it sears steaks adequately — not as hot as a dedicated gas grill, but hot enough for proper browning. At 180°F it cold-smokes fish and cheese. The 575 square inches of cooking area fits 24 burgers, 5 racks of ribs, or 4 whole chickens simultaneously. The included meat probe monitors internal food temperature without opening the lid, which is critical for maintaining consistent temperature during low-and-slow cooks like brisket and pork shoulder. Baking bread, pizza, and even desserts in the Pro 575 produces results with a subtle smoke character that no oven can replicate.

Traeger Pro 575 vs Pro 780 — Which to Buy at Prime Day?

Both grills share identical cooking technology and produce the same quality results — the only real difference is capacity.

Traeger Pro 575 — Pellet Costs and Running Expenses

Traeger Signature pellets cost $19-23 per 20-pound bag at regular price and drop to $15-18 at Prime Day. A 3-hour cook at 225°F uses approximately 1-2 pounds of pellets; a high-heat 1-hour cook uses 2-3 pounds. For weekly weekend cooking the pellet cost runs $5-10 per session — comparable to a tank of propane for a gas grill but with significantly more flavor complexity. Third-party pellets from CookinPellets, BBQrs Delight, and Pit Boss are compatible with the Pro 575 at lower prices — $15-18 per 40 pounds — and many pitmasters prefer them for specific flavor profiles.

What pellets does the Traeger Pro 575 use?+
The Pro 575 uses standard 1-inch compressed hardwood pellets. Traeger sells their own branded pellets in flavors including Signature Blend, Hickory, Apple, Mesquite, Cherry, and Pecan. Third-party pellets from brands like CookinPellets, BBQrs Delight, and Pit Boss are fully compatible and often cheaper. Traeger's warranty does not require the use of Traeger-branded pellets.
How long does the Traeger Pro 575 take to heat up?+
The Traeger Pro 575 reaches 350°F in approximately 15 minutes from cold. For high-heat grilling at 450°F allow 20-25 minutes. For low-and-slow smoking at 180-225°F the grill stabilizes in 10-12 minutes. The startup procedure involves a 5-minute ignition cycle where the auger feeds pellets to the fire pot before the fan ignites them — this is normal and not a malfunction.
