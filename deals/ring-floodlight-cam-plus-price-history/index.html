💡 What You Actually Get at Full PriceThe Floodlight Cam Plus delivers 1080p HD video, two motion-activated LED floodlights rated at 2,000 lumens, a 105dB built-in siren, two-way talk, and customizable motion zones — all controllable through the free Ring app without any required subscription. It's a genuinely capable hardwired security camera; the issue isn't the product, it's the timing of the purchase.

What Hardwired Installation Actually Involves

Unlike Ring's battery-powered cameras, the Floodlight Cam Plus needs to be hardwired to an existing electrical junction box — typically replacing an outdoor floodlight fixture you already have. If you're comfortable with basic fixture swaps, this is a same-afternoon DIY project. If not, Ring's installation partners or a local electrician can handle it, though that adds to the total cost of ownership beyond the camera price itself.

Plus vs. Pro: Which One Do You Actually Need

The step-up Floodlight Cam Pro adds radar-powered 3D Motion Detection, Bird's Eye View motion path tracking, sharper Retinal 4K video with 10x zoom, a louder 110dB siren, and dual-band WiFi — for a meaningfully higher price. For most driveway or backyard coverage use cases, the Plus model's 1080p video and standard motion zones cover the core job; the Pro's extras matter most for larger properties or anyone who wants precise motion-path data rather than a simple triggered alert.

✅ Who Should Buy Now vs. WaitBuy now only if you have an active, specific security need — a break-in attempt, a new move-in with no existing coverage, or a similar urgent situation where waiting isn't practical. For everyone else, this is a textbook wait-for-the-sale product: the discount pattern is consistent, predictable, and worth roughly $80–$90 in savings if you can hold off until the next major event.

Maintenance Tips

Clean the camera lens and floodlight covers periodically, especially after storms or pollen season, since buildup can noticeably reduce night vision clarity and motion detection accuracy. Check the mounting bracket's weatherproofing seal annually if you're in a region with heavy rain or snow, as a compromised seal is the most common cause of premature failure in outdoor hardwired cameras.
