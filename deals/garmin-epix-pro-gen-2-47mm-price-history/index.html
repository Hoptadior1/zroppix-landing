What the Epix Pro (Gen 2) is

The Garmin Epix Pro (Gen 2) is a premium multisport smartwatch with an AMOLED display, multi-band GPS and Garmin's training and health features. It comes in three case sizes, 42mm, 47mm and 51mm, and in standard and Sapphire editions. The 47mm is the mid-size option, and it is the one this page is about.

It launched in 2023, and Garmin has since released newer flagship lines such as the Fenix 8. A common pattern with older flagships is that retailers mark down remaining stock when a newer line arrives. Whether that applies to this watch today is exactly what a price history can show, so we don't assume it.

Which size and edition?

Size and edition change the price and the battery life, so decide these before you hunt for a deal.

  • 42mm: the smallest, suited to smaller wrists.
  • 47mm: the mainstream size, a balance of screen size and comfort.
  • 51mm: the largest, with the biggest screen and, per Garmin's specs, longer battery life.
  • Standard vs Sapphire: Sapphire editions add a scratch-resistant lens and cost more. Check which edition a listing is for.

Garmin lists battery life separately for each size and for always-on display, so check the figures for the exact size you're buying on Garmin's own page.

Who should buy it, and who shouldn't

It suits runners, hikers and outdoor athletes who want a bright AMOLED screen, detailed training data and multi-day battery in a smartwatch.

Look elsewhere if you mostly want smart features such as apps and voice (an Apple Watch or Galaxy Watch may fit better), you want a lighter or cheaper running watch, or you'd rather have Garmin's newer line. Compare the alternatives below.

Common mistakes when buying a premium watch

  • Buying the wrong size. Wrist fit matters more than a few extra millimetres of screen.
  • Mixing up editions. A listing for a standard edition can look similar to a Sapphire one at a very different price.
  • Trusting a crossed-out price. Compare against the recorded price history, not the list price.
  • Ignoring newer models. If a newer watch costs only a little more, it may be the better buy.
  • Forgetting accessories. Extra bands and chargers can add to the real cost.
