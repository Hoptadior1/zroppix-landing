Who sells it matters as much as the price

The $9.78 price was from a third-party seller, not Phomemo or Amazon itself. That can be fine, but check before you pay:

  • The "Sold by" and "Ships from" lines. Phomemo or Amazon is the safest. A third party needs a closer look at its rating and recent reviews.
  • The code. Promo codes are applied at checkout and can disappear. Confirm the final price in the cart before you pay.
  • The return window. Make sure you can send it back if it arrives faulty.
  • Phomemo's own site. It runs separate pricing. Compare before you buy.

The real cost is the rolls, not the printer

The D30 uses direct thermal printing, so there is no ink or toner. That is a genuine saving, but the labels themselves are a consumable. Add the price of spare rolls to your total, and check which roll width the D30 takes before you buy a multi-pack. It prints in a single colour on whatever stock you load, and it works through Phomemo's phone app over Bluetooth, so check the app is available for your phone.

How to tell whether today's price is fair

We don't publish a price chart or a predicted price for the Phomemo D30 on this page, because we can't back one up with recorded data here. What we can give you is a method that works for any product, and the free Zroppix extension that applies it for you on the Amazon page.

  1. Confirm the exact version. Model year, size, colour and bundle can each change the price. Read the title and the variant selector, not just the headline.
  2. Open the price history. Use the Zroppix extension (90-day history on Amazon pages) or another tracker such as CamelCamelCamel or Keepa. Where Amazon shows its own price history link, use that too.
  3. Compare today's price with the lowest in the window. At or near the low is a fair price. A big "percent off" badge on its own proves little, as our guide to sale badges explains.
  4. Check who sells it. Amazon or the brand is safest. Compare against the manufacturer's own site, which may run separate promotions.
  5. Set an alert if the price isn't right yet, and decide on your own timeline. Nobody can tell you what the price will do next.
