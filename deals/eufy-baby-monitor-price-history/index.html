eufy Baby Monitor — Why the Subscription Question Matters More Than the Sticker Price

Most premium baby monitors are priced to look competitive on the shelf, then make up the difference with a required annual subscription for the features that actually make them "smart" — sleep analytics, extended video history, health alerts. eufy's monitors are one of the few options in this category that don't do this: local on-device storage means live viewing, motion/cry detection, and family sharing work fully without a recurring fee.

That doesn't automatically make eufy the right choice for everyone — Nanit and Owlet offer real analytics and health-tracking capability eufy doesn't match. But it does mean the total cost of eufy ownership stays flat for as long as you own it, while subscription-based competitors keep accumulating cost every year.

eufy Baby Monitor Features

Both models include motion, cry, loud-noise, and temperature detection with adjustable sensitivity, two-way audio, and active noise reduction (eufy states up to 20dB) to reduce false alerts from ambient room noise. The hybrid connection model — a physical Wi-Fi on/off switch — lets you run the monitor fully offline on a local connection for privacy, or switch on Wi-Fi for remote app access when you're away from home.

Recorded video is stored locally on-device by default rather than in the cloud, with the option to expand storage via a separate microSD card. This is a meaningful privacy difference from cloud-first competitors, though it also means you're responsible for your own storage rather than relying on a vendor-managed cloud archive.
