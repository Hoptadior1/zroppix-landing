Why laptop deals are easy to get wrong

The same laptop name can cover many configurations that differ in processor, memory, storage and screen, and the price gap between them is large. A listing might advertise a headline price for the base model while the version you actually want costs far more. Sale badges compare against a high list price, and a discounted laptop with too little memory is no bargain. So the question isn't just "is this discounted?" but "is this the spec I need, and is its price low compared with its own history?"

Which kind of laptop do you need?

  • MacBook: strong battery life, build quality and resale value; runs macOS only. Good for most students and professionals, and for anyone already in Apple's ecosystem.
  • Windows laptop: the widest range of prices, sizes and ports, and the best choice for most PC games and some specialist software. Quality varies a lot by brand and model.
  • Chromebook: low cost and simple, good for web, documents and school use, with limited local software.
  • Tablet (such as an iPad): great for reading, notes, drawing and media. Add a keyboard if you type a lot, though it won't replace every laptop job.

The specs that matter

  • Memory (RAM): as a general rule, 16 GB is a comfortable baseline for most people in 2026. 8 GB is for light use and may feel tight. On many modern laptops memory is soldered and can't be upgraded later, so buy what you need now.
  • Storage: 512 GB is a sensible starting point for most people. 256 GB fills up quickly with photos and apps.
  • Processor generation: newer chips are more efficient, but last generation's chip is often plenty and much cheaper.
  • Screen: check size, resolution and brightness. A dim or low-resolution display is the one thing you can't fix.
  • Battery: compare real-world reviews rather than the maker's figure.
  • Ports and weight: check for the ports you actually use, and that it's light enough to carry.
  • Keyboard and trackpad: you touch them all day. Reviews help.

Configuration traps on Amazon

Laptop listings often group several configurations on one page, and Amazon may show the cheapest by default. Before you pay, check that the selected option shows the memory, storage, colour and size you want. Look for words like "renewed" or "refurbished", which mean something different from new. Check the seller too: a laptop sold and shipped by Amazon or the brand is the safest choice, and the return policy matters more than for small items.

The older-model strategy

When a new laptop generation launches, the previous one often drops in price while stock lasts. For many people, last generation's laptop is the best value, because the practical difference between generations is often small. Our M4 vs M5 comparison shows how to think about it. Check the price history to see whether the discount is real, and check how long the manufacturer will keep supporting it.

If you're buying for school or college

Students need reliability, battery life and a keyboard they can type on for hours more than they need raw speed. A mid-range laptop with 16 GB of memory and decent storage usually serves a degree better than a cheaper machine that struggles by the second year. Check whether your course needs specific software (some courses require Windows-only programs), look at the warranty and accidental-damage options, and buy a case. We go deeper in our college laptop and tablet guide. Back-to-school promotions can be good, but the same price-history check applies.

New, renewed or refurbished?

On Amazon, "new" means unused. "Renewed" and "refurbished" mean the item has been used or repaired, and warranty and battery condition vary by seller and programme. A good refurbished laptop can be excellent value, but only if the seller, the condition grade, the warranty and the return policy are clear. If any of those are vague, choose new. Make sure the price history you check is for the same condition, because new and renewed prices are tracked differently.

Protecting your purchase

  • Buy a sleeve or case on day one.
  • Keep the box and receipt through the return window.
  • Register the warranty with the manufacturer.
  • Back up regularly, and don't rely on one drive.
  • Keep the system updated, and use the charger the maker supplies or an approved one.

Laptops on Black Friday

Laptops and tablets are heavily promoted around Black Friday (Friday, November 27, 2026). Decide on the configuration first, note the price history of that exact model, and buy only if the price is at or near the lowest it has been in the past year. Beware of special "sale" models with lower specs than the standard version. Our Black Friday plan has the full checklist.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying too little memory or storage to hit a price.
  • Trusting the headline price without checking the configuration.
  • Ignoring the screen and keyboard.
  • Buying a refurbished unit by accident.
  • Skipping the warranty and return terms.
  • Paying a premium for a chip you won't use. Match the machine to your work.

Laptop buying checklist

Do these before you pay. They matter more for laptops than for almost anything else.

  • Decide the use case and the type that fits.
  • Pick the memory and storage you need now.
  • Confirm the exact configuration on the listing.
  • Open the price history and find the lowest price in the past year.
  • Compare today's price with that low.
  • Check it's new, not renewed, unless you intend that.
  • Check the seller, warranty and return window.

Frequently asked questions

How much RAM do I need in a laptop?

As a general rule, 16 GB is comfortable for most people. 8 GB suits light use. Many laptops can't be upgraded, so buy what you need now.

How much storage do I need?

512 GB is a sensible starting point. 256 GB fills quickly with photos and apps.

MacBook or Windows laptop?

MacBooks are strong on battery and build quality, and Windows laptops offer more choice and the widest software and gaming support. Pick by the software you need.

Is last year's laptop worth buying?

Often yes. The difference is small for many people and the price is lower. Check the history and the exact configuration.

How do I check a laptop deal is real?

Open the price history for that exact configuration and compare today's price with its lowest in the past year.

What does renewed mean on Amazon?

Renewed means used or refurbished, not new. Check the condition and warranty terms before buying.

Should I buy a laptop on Black Friday?

Only if the price for the configuration you want is at or near its yearly low. Decide in advance.

Can an iPad replace a laptop?

For reading, notes and media, often yes. For heavy typing, file management or specialist software, a laptop is easier.

Buy the right spec at a real price

Choose the configuration that fits your work, then buy when its price is genuinely low compared with its own history.
