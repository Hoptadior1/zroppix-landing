Installation Considerations

Most 4-channel kits like this power the front camera off the 12V cigarette lighter port by default, but running a genuine 24-hour parking mode reliably requires hardwiring directly to the vehicle's fuse box — a separate kit, sometimes included, sometimes purchased separately depending on the specific bundle. Without hardwiring, parking mode will typically only run until the standard 12V port loses power when the car is off, which on many vehicles is immediate.

The interior/rear cameras usually route through the cabin along the headliner and A-pillar trim, which is a more involved install than a simple front-camera-only dash cam. Budget extra time for a first install, or consider professional installation if hardwiring and interior wire-routing aren't something you're comfortable doing yourself — this is standard advice across the multi-channel dash cam category, not specific to this model.

Who Actually Needs Full 360° Coverage

Rideshare and delivery drivers benefit most from the interior-facing channel, since it protects against liability disputes with passengers. Anyone who parks in dense urban areas or apartment complexes benefits from genuine rear coverage, given how common parking-lot dings and hit-and-run scrapes are compared to on-road accidents. A front-only camera remains the right, cheaper choice for drivers mainly concerned with on-road incidents and less worried about parking-related damage or passenger disputes.
