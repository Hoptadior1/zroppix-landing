What SimpliSafe is, and why kits vary

SimpliSafe makes wireless DIY home-security systems: a base station, sensors for doors, windows and motion, a keypad, and optional cameras, sold in numbered kits such as the "8-piece" system. You place the sensors yourself and control the system from a keypad or the app. Professional monitoring is optional and sold by the month, according to SimpliSafe's public information.

The key thing to know before you shop is that kits vary. A numbered kit's contents (how many entry sensors, motion sensors and extras) can change between bundles and over time, and Amazon listings may differ from SimpliSafe's own site. Read the item list on the exact listing, not just the "8-piece" label.

Monitoring plans: check before you buy the hardware

The hardware price is only part of the cost. SimpliSafe's monitoring plans, which add professional dispatch and in some tiers video verification, are billed monthly, and the features available without a plan can differ by hardware and by plan. SimpliSafe sets the plan names and prices and changes them from time to time, so we don't quote figures here.

  1. Open SimpliSafe's current plans page and read what each tier includes.
  2. Check what works without a plan, such as local alarm and app control, and what needs one, such as cellular connectivity or camera recording.
  3. Add up the cost over the time you'll own it, not just the kit price.
  4. Check the terms for cancelling or pausing.

Is it a good fit for renters and first-time buyers?

A wireless system is easier to install than a wired one and doesn't need an installer. Many sensors attach with adhesive, which suits rented homes, but you should check your lease and the mounting method for each part. SimpliSafe publishes setup guides and support, so check the current ones before you start. For a house with many doors and windows, count them first, because a kit may not cover every entry point.

Common mistakes when buying a security system

  • Buying by kit name. Two "8-piece" kits may not hold the same sensors.
  • Forgetting the monthly cost. A cheap kit with an expensive plan can cost more than a pricier kit.
  • Under-counting entry points. Count doors, ground-floor windows and areas for motion sensors before you pick a kit.
  • Trusting a "50% off" badge. Compare against recorded price history.
  • Buying from an unknown seller. Choose Amazon or SimpliSafe for a security product.
