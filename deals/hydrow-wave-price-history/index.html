📊 Part of a Real Shift Toward Home Cardio Replacing Gym Memberships

Home rowing machines are riding a genuine, sustained trend of consumers skipping gym memberships in favor of owning premium cardio equipment outright — not a passing fad. Most smart rowing machines in this connected-fitness category run over $2,000, making the Wave's $1,695 price point a real value entry rather than a stripped-down budget option.

What You're Actually Giving Up for $500 Less

Independent testers who put the Wave through a month of use directly compared it against the original Hydrow and found the difference isn't really a sacrifice for most home users — the Wave trades a bulkier, heavier build for a more compact, easily portable footprint. For anyone without dedicated triple-digit square footage for a home gym, that's an upgrade, not a downgrade, and testers concluded the rowing experience itself doesn't meaningfully suffer for it.

💡 A Real Alternative to the Concept2, Not Just a Hydrow ProductTesters who evaluated the Wave specifically called it a genuine alternative to the Concept2 RowErg — a longtime favorite that consistently tops cardio equipment guides. That's meaningful context: the Wave isn't just competing within Hydrow's own product line, it's being positioned credibly against the established gold standard in home rowing.

The Membership Question — What You're Actually Buying

The Wave includes a limited "Just Row" mode without a subscription, but the full experience — on-demand and live class libraries, HydroMetrics performance tracking, and personalized workout recommendations — requires the separate Hydrow All-Access membership, priced around $50/month. This mirrors the connected-fitness membership model of Peloton and similar platforms; the hardware price alone doesn't capture the full ongoing cost.

HydroMetrics Tracks More Than Just Calories

Hydrow's intelligent training system tracks the balance of Precision, Power, and Endurance across every stroke, giving specific technique feedback (like "try driving more with your legs to improve your split") rather than just raw distance and calorie numbers. This turns each session into structured skill-building rather than just logging generic cardio output.

✅ Who Should Buy ThisThis is a strong fit for anyone genuinely committed to replacing gym cardio sessions with a home rowing routine, especially in space-constrained homes where the original Hydrow's bulk is a real obstacle. If you specifically want a no-subscription, purely mechanical rowing experience, a Concept2 RowErg is the better fit — you'll pay for the Hydrow experience through the ongoing membership either way.

What to Do Right Now

At $1,695, this is Hydrow's stable direct price with no active discount cycle — the real savings case here is against the pricier original Hydrow, not against a temporary markdown on the Wave itself. If a compact home cardio machine with genuine instructor-led classes fits your routine, there's little reason to wait.

Setup Tips

Confirm you have enough floor clearance for the full rowing stroke length before ordering, even with the Wave's more compact footprint — the seat rail still needs a real, uninterrupted straight-line path. Use Hydrow's 30-day home trial (available on direct purchases) to confirm the workout style genuinely fits your routine before committing long-term to the membership.
