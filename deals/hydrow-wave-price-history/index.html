What changed since our last update

The page we replaced said the Wave was $1,695, "$500 cheaper" than the Origin. Hydrow's own page now lists the Wave from $1,995, or $42 a month, and Hydrow's blog (September 15, 2025) lists the Origin at $2,195 and the Arc at $2,295 MSRP, so the gap to the Origin is now about $200, not $500. Hydrow's page also lists a free 30-day home trial, a one-year warranty, free shipping and HSA/FSA eligibility. These are the maker's own figures, and Hydrow's blog says its prices "may change with sales and promotions."

The price can move a lot by seller

Reported prices for the Wave have been far from the list price. Slickdeals (July 16, 2026) posted $797.99 at Best Buy against $1,995, and the deal is marked expired. In 2023, Live Science reported $1,445 at Amazon against a $1,895 list price. Commenters on the Slickdeals post say a membership is needed for most functions, so a low hardware price doesn't remove the monthly cost.

The lesson is to compare sellers and check the recorded history before paying list price. We can't tell you what the price is today.

What it costs to own, using Hydrow's numbers

Hydrow lists the membership at $50 a month, or $600 a year prepaid, plus tax, with a $19.99 monthly Digital Basic plan that doesn't need a rower (Hydrow blog, September 15, 2025). Our arithmetic on those figures, before tax and at the $1,995 list price:

  • Year one: $1,995 + $600 = $2,595.
  • Three years: $1,995 + $1,800 = $3,795.

Hydrow's page says the membership covers the whole household. Live Science's 2023 review said the Wave's "Just Row" mode is limited without a membership, and suggested a traditional rower for people who don't want one. Check Hydrow's current terms.

How to tell whether today's price is fair

We don't publish a price chart or a predicted price for the Hydrow Wave on this page, because we can't back one up with recorded data here. What we can give you is a method that works for any product, and the free Zroppix extension that applies it for you on the Amazon page.

  1. Confirm the exact version. Model year, size, colour and bundle can each change the price. Read the title and the variant selector, not just the headline.
  2. Open the price history. Use the Zroppix extension (90-day history on Amazon pages) or another tracker such as CamelCamelCamel or Keepa. Where Amazon shows its own price history link, use that too.
  3. Compare today's price with the lowest in the window. At or near the low is a fair price. A big "percent off" badge on its own proves little, as our guide to sale badges explains.
  4. Check who sells it. Amazon or the brand is safest. Compare against the manufacturer's own site, which may run separate promotions.
  5. Set an alert if the price isn't right yet, and decide on your own timeline. Nobody can tell you what the price will do next.

Who should look at it

  • You want guided classes at home: the membership is the product.
  • You want a rower without a subscription: compare a mechanical rower such as the Concept2 RowErg.
  • You are short on space: Hydrow calls the Wave compact, with optional upright storage. Live Science (2023) said it needs a wall bracket to stand upright, so check the current setup.
  • You want the lowest price: compare sellers and watch the history.

Common mistakes when buying a connected rower

  • Counting only the hardware price. Add the membership.
  • Trusting the old "$500 cheaper" claim. The gap is smaller now.
  • Buying from one seller without comparing. Prices have differed a lot by seller.
  • Skipping the trial terms. Hydrow lists a 30-day home trial, but read the return policy.
