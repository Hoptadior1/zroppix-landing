What's New on the AirTag Gen 2

Amazon shoppers consistently highlight one specific improvement: the AirTag Gen 2 is significantly louder when triggered to play a sound through the Find My app, making a lost item meaningfully easier to locate by ear compared to the original AirTag. Reviewers and outlets like Macworld have also praised the easier setup process and more reliable functionality.

Who Should Buy AirTag Gen 2 vs Alternatives

AirTag Gen 2 is the clear pick for anyone deep in the Apple ecosystem — the precision finding feature specifically requires an iPhone with U1/U2 chip support, and the Find My network's massive device count (far larger than any competing tracker network) means lost items are found faster in practice. Android users should look at Google-ecosystem trackers instead, since AirTag's core precision-finding advantage doesn't extend to Android devices, though Google's unknown tracker alerts can still detect a nearby AirTag for safety purposes.

Common Uses for AirTag Gen 2

Beyond the obvious keys-and-wallet use case, AirTag Gen 2 sees genuine practical use in luggage tracking for travelers wanting peace of mind on flights, backpack tracking for students, and pet collar attachment for cats and small dogs (though Apple doesn't officially market it for pet tracking, and dedicated pet trackers with GPS offer better range for larger dogs that roam further). The lightweight, coin-sized design and one-year battery life make it practical to attach semi-permanently to items you don't want to think about again until the low-battery alert arrives.

Setting Up an AirTag for the First Time

Setup takes under a minute for anyone with an iPhone — simply hold the AirTag near the phone, and a setup card appears automatically prompting you to name the tag and assign it to an item type. Once configured, the tag appears in the Find My app's Items tab alongside any other trackers, showing last known location, distance, and precision-finding directions when nearby. No app store download or account creation beyond an existing Apple ID is required.

Privacy and Anti-Stalking Protections

Apple built specific anti-stalking safeguards into the AirTag system given real-world concerns about covert tracking — an unrecognized AirTag traveling with a person who isn't its owner triggers an automatic alert on their iPhone, and after a period of separation from its paired owner, the tag begins emitting an audible sound to reveal its presence. Android users benefit from Google's parallel "unknown tracker alerts" system, which can detect and flag a nearby AirTag even without an iPhone, extending this safety protection across both major mobile ecosystems rather than just Apple's own users.

Because it works across an entire household once paired to a shared Family Sharing group, gifting a 4-pack to family members who share Apple IDs under one family group can extend tracking coverage across multiple people's most commonly lost items in one purchase.
