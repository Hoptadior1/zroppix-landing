The sole types: FG, SG, AG, TF and indoor

The letters on a boot box tell you which surface it is made for. Using the wrong sole is hard on your knees and ankles and wears out the boots.

CodeSurfaceWhat it looks like
FG (firm ground)Natural grass, dry to normalMoulded studs, usually bladed or conical, around 10 to 14
SG (soft ground)Wet, muddy natural grassFewer, longer, often metal-tipped studs. Check venue rules before using them.
AG (artificial grass)Modern 3G/4G artificial pitchesMore, shorter studs to spread pressure and reduce grip lock
TF (turf)Hard, dry or older artificial surfaces and astroturfMany small rubber nubs, low profile
IN / IC (indoor)Indoor courts, futsal, hard gym floorsFlat gum or rubber sole with no studs
MG / multi-groundVaried surfacesCompromise design. Check the brand's description.

Rule of thumb: buy for the surface you play on most. A cheap pair that matches your pitch beats an expensive pair that does not.

Choosing for your real pitch

  • Weekend league on grass: FG. If your pitch is often soggy, ask your league whether SG is allowed.
  • Training on a 3G/4G pitch: AG. Many venues also accept FG, but AG is kinder to knees.
  • Astroturf and hard, old artificial pitches: TF.
  • Five-a-side halls and futsal: indoor shoes.
  • School pitches, car parks and rough ground: TF or a tough multi-ground boot, and expect faster wear.

If you play on grass and artificial turf equally, many players buy two cheaper pairs rather than one expensive compromise. It also means each pair lasts longer.

Venue rulesSome pitches ban metal studs or long studs. If you are unsure, ask the venue before you buy.

Getting the fit right when ordering online

Boots should feel snug, like a firm handshake, with your toes close to the end but not pressed against it. They stretch slightly with wear, and synthetic uppers usually stretch less than leather.

  1. Measure your foot length in the evening, when feet are largest.
  2. Compare with the brand's size chart, not a different brand's.
  3. Read recent reviews for phrases like "runs small", "narrow" or "wide".
  4. Wear the boots indoors on carpet first, so you can still return them if they are wrong.
  5. Check that the listing has a free or easy return policy.

Wide feet can struggle in narrow boots, and it is worth searching specifically for wide-fit options rather than sizing up and hoping.

Leather, synthetic and knit uppers

  • Natural leather (such as kangaroo or calf) moulds to your foot and feels soft. It stretches over time, so buy snug.
  • Synthetic uppers are lighter, cleaner in wet weather and usually stretch less. They are the most common choice and often the best value.
  • Knit uppers feel like a sock and are very light, but may offer less protection and can wear out faster on rough surfaces.

Do you need expensive boots?

Elite boots use lighter, thinner uppers and finer details. Mid-range boots usually give you very similar grip and comfort at a lower price, and often last longer because the materials are tougher. Unless you play at a high level, the correct sole, a good fit and comfort matter far more than the model number or colour. For growing children, buy a sensible pair that fits now and replace it as they grow.

Where to save and where not toSave on the model tier. Do not save on the sole type, the fit or the return policy.

Looking after your boots

  • Knock off mud after each session and wipe the uppers with a damp cloth.
  • Stuff them with newspaper and dry them at room temperature. Do not use a radiator or direct sun, which damage glue and uppers.
  • Do not put them in the washing machine.
  • Carry them in a separate bag so mud does not spread.
  • Check studs for wear. Worn studs on hard ground are a common cause of slips and sore feet.

Before you click Buy

Five checks that prevent almost every boots return.

  • Surface: you know whether you need FG, AG, TF or indoor.
  • Venue rules: metal or long studs are allowed where you play.
  • Size: checked against the brand's chart, with reviews mentioning narrow or wide fit.

Frequently asked questions

What football boots do I need for artificial grass?

AG (artificial grass) boots, or TF turf shoes for hard, older surfaces. FG boots are made for natural grass.

Can I wear FG boots on a 3G or 4G pitch?

Many venues allow it, but AG is gentler on your knees. Some venues restrict certain studs, so check their rules.

What is the difference between AG and TF?

AG has more, shorter studs for modern artificial pitches. TF has many small rubber nubs for hard turf and astroturf.

How tight should football boots fit?

Snug, with your toes near the end but not pressed against it. They stretch slightly with wear.

Are leather boots better than synthetic?

Leather moulds to your foot and feels soft, but stretches. Synthetic is lighter, cleaner in wet weather and usually better value.

Should kids wear the same boots as adults?

Kids need boots that suit their pitch and fit now. Avoid buying too big, and check the youth size chart.

Can I wear football boots for running or walking?

They are designed for football surfaces. Wearing studs on hard ground such as roads wears them down and can be uncomfortable.

How often should I replace boots?

When the studs are worn, the upper is splitting, or they no longer fit. Heavy weekly use can mean a season or less.
