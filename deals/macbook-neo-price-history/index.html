MacBook Neo Price History — The Full Story

The MacBook Neo launched on March 11, 2026 at $599 — the lowest price Apple has ever put on a Mac. Demand immediately overwhelmed supply: the laptop sold out globally within 48 hours, Apple limited purchases to two units per person, and shipping delays stretched to 2–3 weeks by April. Despite the demand surge, the retail price held at $599 for the first three months of its life.

Price history from tracking services shows the MacBook Neo reached its all-time low of $590 during Amazon Prime Day 2026 (June 23-26) — a modest $9 discount from launch price, but meaningful given Apple's strict pricing discipline. Then, on June 25 — in the middle of Prime Day — Apple raised the MacBook Neo price by $100 to $699, citing global memory shortages. Macworld's Macbook Neo Prime Day coverage called it the best deal of Prime Day immediately because Amazon's $590 stock was now $109 below Apple's new official price. That stock sold through rapidly. Today, $699 is the new normal.

What Is the MacBook Neo

The MacBook Neo is Apple's most affordable Mac ever — a 13-inch laptop starting at $699 (post-hike) powered by the A18 Pro chip from the iPhone 16 Pro. It is Apple's first Mac to use an A-series chip instead of the M-series found in MacBook Air and MacBook Pro, making it simultaneously the cheapest and the most unusual Mac in the lineup. The result is a machine with strong single-core performance, exceptional battery life, and full macOS — at a price that finally competes with Chromebooks and Windows laptops in the $500-700 category.

Apple designed the Neo specifically for students, first-time Mac buyers, and everyday users who want macOS but cannot justify the MacBook Air's $1,099 starting price. The trade-offs vs the Air are real: the Neo has 8GB RAM (non-upgradeable), USB-C only (no Thunderbolt 4, no MagSafe), an sRGB display instead of the Air's P3 wide color, and no backlit keyboard on the base model. For users who spend their time in a browser, Google Docs, Zoom, and Spotify, these trade-offs are essentially invisible in daily use.

💡 The A18 Pro Is Not a DowngradeThe MacBook Neo's A18 Pro chip is not a budget processor — it is the same chip that powers the iPhone 16 Pro, Apple's flagship phone. In CPU benchmarks, the A18 Pro in the MacBook Neo outperforms the M1 MacBook Air by nearly 2x in multi-core tasks. The trade-off vs the M4 in the MacBook Air M4 is real — the M4 is faster and more power-efficient in sustained workloads — but for everyday use, the difference is imperceptible. The 16-core Neural Engine enables the full suite of Apple Intelligence features on macOS Tahoe, which the Neo runs natively.

MacBook Neo vs MacBook Air M4 — Which to Buy

The MacBook Air M4 starts at $849 after its own Prime Day discount (regular $999) — currently $150 more than the Neo at $699. The Air's advantages over the Neo are meaningful and add up: the M4 chip is 25-35% faster than the A18 Pro in sustained workloads, the Air has 16GB base RAM (vs 8GB) which makes a real difference in multitasking, the display is P3 wide color for noticeably better color accuracy, and MagSafe + Thunderbolt 4 dramatically improve the connectivity story.

The decision comes down to budget and use case. For a student who primarily uses a web browser, Google Docs, and Zoom — the Neo at $620-650 delivers 90% of the experience at 70% of the price. For a creative, developer, or anyone who opens more than 15 browser tabs simultaneously — the Air M4 at $849 is the correct choice and the $200 premium is justified by the RAM headroom alone.

MacBook Neo Build Quality and Repairability

The MacBook Neo is built from 90% recycled aluminum — Apple's highest recycled content in any product. iFixit's teardown called it Apple's most repairable laptop in 14 years, noting a screwed-down battery tray, no parts pairing requirements, a screwed-down keyboard, and modular ports and speakers. The storage (but not RAM) can be upgraded with the right tools — a meaningful departure from Apple's recent practice of fully bonding all components. At $699 with a 1-year Apple warranty and a product that iFixit says can be serviced, the Neo's long-term ownership cost compares favorably to Windows laptops in its price range that typically degrade faster and cost more to repair.
