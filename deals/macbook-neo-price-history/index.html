What Is the MacBook Neo

The MacBook Neo is Apple's most affordable Mac — a 13-inch laptop powered by the A18 Pro chip, the same processor found in the iPhone 16 Pro. It is Apple's first Mac to use an A-series chip instead of an M-series chip, making it both the cheapest and most unusual Mac in the current lineup.

Apple positioned the Neo for students, first-time Mac buyers, and everyday users who want macOS at a lower price than the MacBook Air. Real trade-offs versus the Air: 8GB RAM (non-upgradeable), USB-C only (no Thunderbolt 4, no MagSafe), and an sRGB display instead of P3 wide color. For browser-centric, document, and video-call use, these trade-offs are largely invisible day to day.

The A18 Pro is not a stripped-down chip — it's the same silicon powering Apple's flagship iPhone. The Neo's 16-hour battery life is its standout practical advantage, delivering full-day use without needing a charger.

MacBook Neo vs MacBook Air — Which to Buy

The MacBook Air starts meaningfully higher than the Neo. The Air's advantages: a faster M-series chip, more base RAM, MagSafe charging, Thunderbolt 4, and a brighter P3 display. For a student who mainly uses a browser, docs, and video calls, the Neo covers most of that experience at a lower price. For anyone doing heavier multitasking or creative work, the Air's extra RAM and performance headroom are worth the premium.
