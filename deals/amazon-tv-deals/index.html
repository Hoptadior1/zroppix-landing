Why TVs are the best place to use price history

TVs are expensive, they're bought rarely, and their prices move a lot. A model can sit at one price for weeks, drop sharply during a sale, then drift back up. Retailers also compare sale prices to a high list price, so a "35% off" badge can describe a normal price. That combination makes TVs the product where a one-minute price check saves the most money.

The method is simple: find the product's lowest price in the past year, compare it to today's price, and decide using that, not the badge. We cover the method in detail in our Black Friday guide, and compare the tools that show the history in our price-tracker comparison.

OLED, QLED, Mini LED: what's the difference?

The labels describe how the picture is lit and coloured. In plain terms:

  • OLED pixels make their own light and can switch fully off, so blacks are very deep and contrast is excellent. OLEDs are usually best in dim rooms and tend to be less bright than the brightest LCD TVs.
  • QLED is a Samsung and TCL-style name for an LCD TV with a quantum-dot layer for richer colour. It needs a backlight, so it can get very bright.
  • Mini LED describes a backlight made of many tiny LEDs, allowing finer brightness control and stronger highlights than a basic LED backlight. Many Mini LED sets are also QLED.
  • Standard LED/LCD is the budget tier: fine for casual viewing, with weaker contrast.

Names are marketing as much as engineering, so read reviews of the exact model. A good Mini LED set can beat a poor OLED, and the reverse.

Choosing the size

The most common regret is buying too small. As a rough rule for 4K TVs, a comfortable viewing distance is about one to one and a half times the screen's diagonal. A 55-inch TV (roughly 1.4 metres diagonal) suits sitting around 1.4 to 2.1 metres away, and a 65-inch (about 1.65 m) suits around 1.65 to 2.5 metres. If you sit further away, go bigger. Measure the wall or TV unit, including the stand width, before you decide.

Larger sizes also tend to give you more screen per dollar, though the price gap between sizes varies by model and sale, which is why checking the history for the exact size matters. Some lines use different panels in different sizes, so don't assume a review of the 65-inch applies to the 55-inch.

The specs that matter (and the ones that don't)

  • Panel type and brightness: the biggest factor in how the picture looks in your room. Bright room: look at bright LCDs. Dark room: OLED shines.
  • Refresh rate (60 Hz or 120 Hz): 120 Hz helps with fast motion and games. Check the native rate, not an "effective" or "motion rate" figure.
  • HDMI ports and version: if you have a games console or soundbar, check HDMI 2.1 and eARC support.
  • HDR formats: HDR10 is standard. Dolby Vision and HDR10+ are extras that depend on the TV and your sources.
  • Smart platform: make sure your streaming apps are available, or plan a streaming stick.
  • Viewing angle and glare: matter if people sit off to the side or the room has windows.
  • Sound: thin TVs sound thin. Budget for a soundbar.

Ignore marketing numbers that aren't a measured spec, such as made-up "motion rate" or "Ultra" labels. Reviews and the spec table on the listing are more useful.

The model-year trick

TV makers release new models each year, usually in the spring. When a new model arrives, the previous year's model often drops in price while stock remains. If the older model's specs are close, that's one of the best ways to save, and price history shows you when it has happened. The risk is stock: older models eventually run out or return at odd prices. Check the history for the exact model number and the seller before buying a clearance model.

Buying a TV on Black Friday

TVs are among the products most advertised around Black Friday (Friday, November 27, 2026), so the pressure is real. Before then, shortlist two or three models, note each one's lowest price in the past year, and decide your "yes" price. On the day, buy only if the price is at or near that low. Common TV buying mistakes

  • Judging by the sale badge. Check the history.
  • Buying the wrong size variant. Confirm the size and model number on the page.
  • Ignoring the stand. Wide feet need wide furniture, or a wall mount.
  • Forgetting sound. A soundbar is often the best upgrade after the TV itself.
  • Skipping the return policy. Large items can arrive damaged. Know how returns work before you order.
  • Buying a TV with no sources. Check you have the streaming apps or devices you need.

TV buying checklist

Five minutes here can save a hundred dollars or more.

  • Measure the space, the stand width and your seating distance.
  • Pick the panel type that matches your room's light.
  • Confirm the exact model number and size on the listing.
  • Open the price history and find the lowest price in the past year.
  • Compare today's price with that low.
  • Check the ports (HDMI 2.1, eARC) if you have a console or soundbar.
  • Check the seller and return policy before paying.
  • Budget for a soundbar or mount if you need one.

Frequently asked questions

Is OLED better than QLED?

Neither is better in every situation. OLED has deeper blacks and suits dim rooms. QLED and Mini LED sets are usually brighter and suit bright rooms.

What size TV should I buy?

A rough rule is to sit about one to one and a half times the diagonal away. A 55-inch suits about 1.4 to 2.1 metres, and a 65-inch about 1.65 to 2.5 metres.

When do TV prices drop?

They often drop when a new model-year arrives and during big sales, but timing varies by model. Check the price history instead of guessing.

How do I know a TV deal is real?

Open the price history and compare today's price with the lowest price in the past year. At or near that low is a real deal.

Do I need 120 Hz?

It helps with fast motion and games, especially on a console. For casual viewing 60 Hz is fine. Check the native refresh rate on the listing.

Do I need HDMI 2.1?

Only if you want features like 4K at 120 Hz from a games console or eARC for a soundbar. Check the ports.

Is it better to buy last year's model?

Often it's the best value, if the specs suit you and the seller is reliable. Check the model number and history.

Should I buy a TV on Black Friday?

Only if the price is at or near its yearly low. Do your research beforehand and decide your price in advance.

Do I need a soundbar?

Not required, but most thin TVs have weak speakers, so a soundbar is a worthwhile upgrade.

Buy the TV, not the sale badge

Match the panel to your room, check the yearly low, and buy when the price is genuinely good.
