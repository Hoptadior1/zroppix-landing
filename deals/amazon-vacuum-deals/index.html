Which kind of vacuum fits you?

Most bad vacuum purchases come from picking the wrong type, not the wrong brand. Decide the type first, then compare models.

  • Robot vacuums suit flat floors and busy households. They keep floors clear day to day but won't replace a deep clean on thick carpet or stairs.
  • Cordless sticks suit quick clean-ups, stairs and small to medium homes. Battery runtime and replacement cost are the trade-offs.
  • Plug-in uprights suit larger homes and carpet, with no battery to wear out, but they're heavier and need an outlet.
  • Spot and carpet cleaners are for stains, pet accidents and upholstery, not everyday vacuuming.

Is today's price fair? A 4-step check

We can't tell you whether vacuum prices will fall further, and nobody honestly can. What you can do is check the facts before you pay:

  1. Open the model's price history (our links below, or the free Zroppix extension on the Amazon page).

  3. If it's well above the low, set an alert and compare other retailers.
  4. Don't wait if you need one now. A small saving isn't worth weeks of a dirty floor.

What actually matters when you compare

  • Ongoing costs. Filters, brushes, bags, mop pads and cleaning solution can add up, and a cheap vacuum with expensive consumables isn't cheap.
  • Suction claims. Manufacturers don't all measure suction the same way, so numbers like "Pa" aren't a reliable way to compare across brands. Reviews and real-world tests tell you more.
  • Navigation (robots). Random-pattern robots clean less systematically than ones that map your home. Check how the robot sees obstacles if you have cables or pet accidents.
  • Dust bin and base size. A small bin means more emptying, and a big base needs floor space.
  • Noise. Reviews usually mention it, and it matters for apartments.
  • Warranty and support. Check the length, and who handles repairs.

How to spot a fake vacuum discount

Vacuums are a category where "was" prices can mislead, because model numbers and bundles vary so much. A listing that says "40% off" may be comparing against a list price the product rarely sold for. The UK consumer group Which? found in 2021 that 86% of 214 Black Friday deals it tracked were the same price or cheaper in the six months before (a UK study, so not a forecast for this year). That's why the check above matters. For more, read how to tell if a sale badge is real.

Should you buy a renewed or open-box vacuum?

Vacuums are among the categories where used or renewed units can make sense, because the mechanics are simple and the parts are replaceable. The battery in cordless models and robots is the main risk. Read our guide to Warehouse, Outlet and Renewed first, and compare the used price with the new item's price history, not with its list price.

Run through this for any vacuum.

  • Type matches your floors and home.
  • Exact model and bundle confirmed on the listing.
