What the Hatch Restore 2 Does — Three Sleep Tools in One Device

The Restore 2 combines three clinically-supported sleep aids: a white noise machine, a sunrise alarm clock, and a warm reading light. White noise masks environmental disruptions — traffic, neighbors, a partner's movement — that fragment sleep without fully waking you, improving sleep continuity particularly in urban environments. The sunrise alarm gradually brightens from deep red through orange to bright white over a customizable 10-60 minute window before your set wake time, triggering your body's natural melatonin reduction and reducing the groggy post-alarm feeling research calls sleep inertia. The reading light uses warm amber tones that do not suppress melatonin the way blue-spectrum screens and overhead lights do, allowing a wind-down reading routine without delaying sleep onset.

Each of these functions has independent clinical support. The combination in a single app-controlled device — with scheduling, content library access, and a physical volume and brightness dial for tactile control without picking up your phone — is what drives the 40,000+ Amazon ratings. At $130-150 on Prime Day it is the most complete bedside sleep device at its price point by a significant margin.

Hatch Restore 2 vs Loftie vs Philips SmartSleep — Which to Buy

The Restore 2's main competitors are the Loftie Clock ($195), the Philips SmartSleep Wake-Up Light ($100-180), and the Amazon Echo used as a white noise source. The Loftie has a devoted following for its design and two-phase alarm system but lacks a reading light and has a smaller speaker than the Restore 2. At $195 standard versus $130-150 at Prime Day for the Hatch, the Loftie does not justify its premium for most buyers. The Philips SmartSleep is the legitimate sunrise alarm alternative — its light quality for sunrise simulation is excellent and clinical evidence is strong, but it has no white noise, no app-scheduled content library, and no reading light. It solves one of the three sleep problems the Hatch Restore 2 addresses. The Amazon Echo is a free white noise source if you already own one but has no sunrise alarm and no reading light — a workaround, not a solution.

For buyers who want a single device that handles sound masking, natural wake, and screen-free evening reading light, the Restore 2 at Prime Day pricing is the best available option at any price below $200.

Hatch Restore 2 Setup, App, and the Subscription Question

Setup takes under 10 minutes via the Hatch Sleep app on iOS or Android. The device connects to your home WiFi and runs independently once routines are programmed — you do not need the app open nightly. The physical dial controls brightness and volume directly without requiring your phone. During WiFi outages the device runs your last-programmed settings from local storage, which matters for a device you rely on every night.

The subscription question comes up constantly: the Hatch+ subscription is $4.99/month or $49.99/year and adds guided sleep meditations, wind-down body scans, and an expanded soundscape library. The free tier includes white noise, rain, ocean waves, fan sounds, the sunrise alarm, the reading light, and full scheduling. The majority of users report the free tier covers everything they need. The subscription is a genuine optional add-on, not a paywall on core functionality. Do not let it deter you from the device itself.
