What changed on September 9, 2026

On September 9, 2026, the day it launched the iPhone 18 Pro line, Apple raised the US price of the iPhone 17 from $799 to $899, a flat $100, according to WhistleOut. Tech-ish and Business Today report the same day's changes: the iPhone Air, iPhone 16 and iPhone 17e also rose $100, and the iPhone 17 Pro and 17 Pro Max left Apple's US store. Tech-ish says Apple issued no press release; the new prices simply appeared on its online store. Tim Cook had told the Wall Street Journal in June 2026 that price increases were "unavoidable" because of memory chip costs, without saying when (reported by Good Morning America, June 18, 2026).

So $899 is now Apple's price. The question for you is whether a retailer's listing beats it. Remaining stock at other sellers may or may not be priced lower, and that is exactly what the live check below tells you.

What you get for $899

From Apple's tech specs page: a 6.3-inch OLED display with ProMotion up to 120Hz, the A19 chip, 256GB or 512GB of storage, a 48MP Fusion main camera and a 48MP Fusion ultra wide camera, an 18MP Center Stage front camera, up to 30 hours of video playback and Ceramic Shield 2 on the front. Colours are Black, White, Mist Blue, Sage and Lavender.

We have not tested it. If a claim here matters to your decision, check the spec on Apple's page.

How to tell whether today's price is fair

We don't publish a price chart or a predicted price for the iPhone 17 on this page, because we can't back one up with recorded data here. What we can give you is a method that works for any product, and the free Zroppix extension that applies it for you on the Amazon page.

  1. Confirm the exact version. Model year, size, colour and bundle can each change the price. Read the title and the variant selector, not just the headline.
  2. Open the price history. Use the Zroppix extension (90-day history on Amazon pages) or another tracker such as CamelCamelCamel or Keepa. Where Amazon shows its own price history link, use that too.
  3. Compare today's price with the lowest in the window. At or near the low is a fair price. A big "percent off" badge on its own proves little, as our guide to sale badges explains.
  4. Check who sells it. Amazon or the brand is safest. Compare against the manufacturer's own site, which may run separate promotions.
  5. Set an alert if the price isn't right yet, and decide on your own timeline. Nobody can tell you what the price will do next.

Who should look at it

  • You want a current iPhone without paying Pro money: the 17 is the lowest-priced current model in the lineup we found, after the 17e and 16.
  • You want a thinner, lighter phone: look at the iPhone Air, at $200 more.
  • You want a 4x telephoto or a bigger battery: compare the 18 Pro at $1,199.
  • You are upgrading from a phone that still works well: there is no rush. Set an alert instead.

Common mistakes when buying

  • Comparing with the old $799 price. Apple's price is $899 now.
  • Buying the wrong storage. The iPhone 17 comes in 256GB and 512GB per Apple.
  • Assuming a carrier deal is cheaper. Read the plan, trade-in and credit terms.
  • Buying from an unknown seller. Check warranty and return window.
