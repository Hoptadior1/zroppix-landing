What's New on the JBL Charge 6

The Charge 6 introduces JBL's new AI Sound Boost technology — a real-time audio processing algorithm that analyzes music as it plays to optimize acoustic performance and reduce distortion, a genuine upgrade over the previous Charge 5's fixed sound profile. It also adds Auracast support, letting owners stereo-pair two Charge 6 units or connect multiple Auracast-enabled JBL speakers together for a wider, synchronized sound stage.

Build quality remains a standout — an IP68 waterproof and dustproof rating (JBL's most rugged tier), drop-resistant construction rated for a 1-meter fall onto concrete, and a redesigned removable carrying strap. The built-in powerbank function, letting the speaker charge a phone or other device via USB while playing music, carries over from previous generations and remains a genuinely useful feature for outdoor use.

JBL Charge 6 vs Flip 7 — Which Size Fits Your Use

The Charge 6 sits in JBL's mid-size tier, offering substantially more volume and bass output than the more compact, pocket-friendly Flip 7, at the cost of a larger, heavier design better suited to being carried in a bag than a pocket. For backyard gatherings, poolside use, or anywhere you want genuinely loud, full sound, the Charge 6's larger drivers are the better choice. For solo travel, hiking, or anywhere portability matters more than maximum volume, the smaller Flip 7 — also currently showing strong search growth — is worth comparing directly before deciding.

Care and Maintenance

Despite the IP68 waterproof rating, rinsing the speaker with fresh water after exposure to saltwater, chlorine, or sand — then allowing it to fully air dry before charging — helps preserve the port seals and charging contacts over years of outdoor use. The removable carrying strap can be detached for washing separately if it picks up dirt or odor from regular outdoor use, a small but genuinely practical design detail carried over and refined from earlier Charge generations.
