Update: the OLED iPad mini is out

Apple announced the new iPad mini with an OLED display and an A20 Pro chip. MIXED News (October 28, 2026) reported it from $699 for 128GB Wi-Fi, shipping November 6. See the OLED iPad mini price page. The prices below are for the older A17 Pro model.

What changed since our last update

The page we replaced told you to wait for a sale price of $399 to $449 and showed a price chart. That chart was generated by the page, not recorded, so we removed it. Here is what the named sources say.

Apple raised US hardware prices in June 2026. The Next Web (July 16, 2026) reports the iPad mini now starts at $599, up from $499. On October 7, 2026, Macworld reported the A17 Pro model with 128GB at $499 on Amazon, $100 below that list price. That was a sale during Amazon's October event, which has ended, so we can't tell you whether $499 is still available.

A new iPad mini is reportedly coming

According to Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, as reported by MacRumors (August 30, 2026), an iPad mini with an OLED display is expected to launch by the end of October. iThinkDiff expects a starting price around $699, and Macworld expects it to cost at least $50 more than today's list price. Apple has not announced it, none of these prices are confirmed, and none of the reports say what would happen to the current model's price.

So the decision comes down to three questions, not a prediction:

  • Do you need a tablet now? If yes, the current mini is a proven product, and a price at or below the $499 Oct 7 report is a fair reference point.
  • Does OLED matter to you? If so, it may be worth waiting to see what Apple announces.
  • Is the price at or near the lowest you've seen? Check the recorded history for the exact model, as below.

What you get with the current iPad mini

Per MacRumors: an 8.3-inch Liquid Retina display (2,266 by 1,488), the A17 Pro chip with 8GB of RAM, USB-C at up to 10Gb/s, 128GB, 256GB or 512GB storage, support for Apple Pencil Pro and the USB-C Apple Pencil, and Apple Intelligence. It launched in October 2024 at $499. (Our old page said the screen was 7.69 inches. That was wrong.)

How to tell whether today's price is fair

We don't publish a price chart or a predicted price for the iPad mini A17 Pro on this page, because we can't back one up with recorded data here. What we can give you is a method that works for any product, and the free Zroppix extension that applies it for you on the Amazon page.

  1. Confirm the exact version. Model year, size, colour and bundle can each change the price. Read the title and the variant selector, not just the headline.
  2. Open the price history. Use the Zroppix extension (90-day history on Amazon pages) or another tracker such as CamelCamelCamel or Keepa. Where Amazon shows its own price history link, use that too.
  3. Compare today's price with the lowest in the window. At or near the low is a fair price. A big "percent off" badge on its own proves little, as our guide to sale badges explains.
  4. Check who sells it. Amazon or the brand is safest. Compare against the manufacturer's own site, which may run separate promotions.
  5. Set an alert if the price isn't right yet, and decide on your own timeline. Nobody can tell you what the price will do next.

Who should look at it

  • You want a pocketable tablet for reading, notes or travel: the mini is the smallest iPad.
  • You want a bigger screen: compare the standard iPad or the Air instead.
  • You want the newest display: watch for Apple's announcement before you pay.
  • You want the lowest price: check the recorded history and set an alert instead of guessing.

Common mistakes when buying an iPad mini

  • Trusting a price chart that isn't recorded. Use a tracker with real history.
  • Buying the wrong storage. 128GB, 256GB and 512GB are priced differently.
  • Treating a sale as the normal price. $499 was reported as a sale against a $599 list price.
  • Assuming a rumoured product will arrive or cost a particular amount. Reports differ and Apple hasn't confirmed.
