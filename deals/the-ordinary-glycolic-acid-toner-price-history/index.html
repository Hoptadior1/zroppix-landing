7% Glycolic Acid — What It Does and Why This Concentration Works

Glycolic acid is an alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) derived from sugarcane that exfoliates by dissolving the bonds between dead skin cells at the surface, allowing them to shed more efficiently. The result is smoother texture, brighter tone, reduced appearance of fine lines, and improved absorption of subsequent skincare products. The Ordinary chose 7% as the concentration for their toning solution because it sits at the sweet spot between visible efficacy and broad tolerability: high enough to deliver real exfoliation with consistent use, low enough that most adults can work up to daily use without significant irritation.

In addition to glycolic acid, The Ordinary's formula includes Tasmanian Pepperberry extract, which helps reduce the irritation and sensitivity that high-concentration AHA products commonly cause. This addition is one of the details that separates The Ordinary's formulation from cheaper glycolic acid products — the inclusion of an active irritation-mitigating ingredient at a price point where competing brands would cut corners. The formula also contains ginseng root extract for brightening and amino acid complex for surface hydration, creating a multi-functional formula rather than a single-active exfoliant.

How to Use The Ordinary Glycolic Acid Toner — The Complete Guide

Apply to a cotton pad and sweep across the face and neck after cleansing, in the evening routine only. Avoid the eye area and any active breakouts or broken skin. Do not rinse after application. Allow 5-10 minutes for absorption before applying serum or moisturizer. The next morning, apply SPF without exception — glycolic acid makes skin significantly more photosensitive and skipping sunscreen while using AHA products is a common mistake that leads to new hyperpigmentation rather than resolving existing discoloration.

For beginners, start at 3 applications per week for the first 2-3 weeks. Mild tingling is normal and usually dissipates within 60 seconds. Persistent burning that does not fade within 2 minutes is a sign to reduce frequency or dilute by applying to a slightly damp face rather than dry skin. Once tolerance is established, daily use is appropriate for most skin types. Do not use on the same nights as retinol, niacinamide above 10%, or other AHA/BHA products — use glycolic acid on alternating nights to avoid over-exfoliation and barrier disruption.
