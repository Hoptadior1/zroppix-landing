⚠️ Why This Isn't a Normal "Wait for a Deal" Situation

Apple CEO Tim Cook told the Wall Street Journal that Apple can no longer absorb rising memory and storage chip costs, calling the shortage a "hundred-year flood" — the worst he's seen in over 40 years. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports the hikes are "imminent," meaning they could hit current flagship models like the iPhone 17 Pro Max at any time, not just at a future launch.

What's New on the iPhone 17 Pro Max

The Pro Max shares every upgrade from the standard Pro — vapor chamber cooling, aluminum unibody, triple 48MP Fusion cameras with a 4x tetraprism lens — while adding the largest screen and battery in the lineup. The 6.9-inch OLED display at 120Hz pairs with a 5,088 mAh battery, Apple's largest ever, delivering up to 14 hours of video playback according to verified reviews.

What to Do Right Now

With a sitting Apple CEO publicly confirming price increases are coming, waiting on the flagship Pro Max carries real, unusual risk — especially given its already-premium starting price means any percentage increase translates to a larger dollar amount than on cheaper models. If the Pro Max's battery and camera capabilities matter to you, today's price is worth locking in.

Pro Max vs Pro — Is the Extra Size Worth It

Beyond the larger 6.9-inch display, the Pro Max's main practical advantage is battery life — the larger chassis allows for a significantly bigger 5,088 mAh cell versus the standard Pro's smaller battery, translating to genuinely longer screen-on time for heavy users. The tradeoff is pure size and weight, since both models share identical camera hardware, chip, and cooling system. For anyone who regularly runs their phone down before day's end, the Pro Max's battery advantage alone often justifies the $100 premium over the standard Pro.
