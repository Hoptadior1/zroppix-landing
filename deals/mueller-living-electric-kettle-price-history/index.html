What buyers complain about

The comments under that Slickdeals post are mixed, and they are the only buyer feedback we cite. One commenter said they had replaced the thermostat or switch twice and that the plastic mesh near the spout had deteriorated. Another said a Mueller bought in 2023 failed without warning. Another said it still looked like it had plastic parts. A fourth said they used one daily for four years with a good experience. These are individual opinions, not test results, and we haven't tested the kettle.

The practical lesson: read the recent reviews on your exact listing, check which parts touch the water, and check the warranty. If you are buying for a dorm, check your residence's appliance policy first, because some halls restrict heating appliances.

How to tell whether today's price is fair

We don't publish a price chart or a predicted price for the Mueller glass electric kettle on this page, because we can't back one up with recorded data here. What we can give you is a method that works for any product, and the free Zroppix extension that applies it for you on the Amazon page.

  1. Confirm the exact version. Model year, size, colour and bundle can each change the price. Read the title and the variant selector, not just the headline.
  2. Open the price history. Use the Zroppix extension (90-day history on Amazon pages) or another tracker such as CamelCamelCamel or Keepa. Where Amazon shows its own price history link, use that too.
  3. Compare today's price with the lowest in the window. At or near the low is a fair price. A big "percent off" badge on its own proves little, as our guide to sale badges explains.
  4. Check who sells it. Amazon or the brand is safest. Compare against the manufacturer's own site, which may run separate promotions.
  5. Set an alert if the price isn't right yet, and decide on your own timeline. Nobody can tell you what the price will do next.
