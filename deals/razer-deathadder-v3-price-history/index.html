What the Razer DeathAdder V3 Actually Is

The DeathAdder line is one of Razer's longest-running gaming mouse designs, and the V3 wired version weighs 59g with a right-handed ergonomic shape developed with input from esports players. It uses Razer's Focus Pro 30K optical sensor and Gen-3 optical switches rated for roughly 90 million clicks, with a woven Speedflex cable designed to minimize drag during fast swipes. It supports polling rates up to 8,000 Hz through Razer's HyperPolling technology and has 6 programmable buttons.

Wired vs. Wireless — What Actually Changes

Razer sells several DeathAdder V3 variants, and it's easy to conflate their specs if you're comparing listings quickly. The wired V3 covered above is 59g. The DeathAdder V3 Pro (wireless) is slightly heavier at 63–64g depending on the specific listing, but keeps the same Focus Pro 30K sensor and Gen-3 optical switches, adding HyperSpeed wireless connectivity and up to 90 hours of battery life. There's also a separate DeathAdder V3 HyperSpeed variant at 55g with a lower-spec 26K sensor and up to 100 hours of battery — a different tier from the Pro, despite the similar name.

The practical takeaway: if raw sensor and switch performance is your priority and you don't mind a cable, the wired V3 gives you the same core tracking hardware as the pricier V3 Pro at a lower weight and (typically) lower price. If you want to eliminate the cable entirely, the V3 Pro keeps the same top-tier sensor; the HyperSpeed variant trades some sensor performance for a lighter wireless build.

Is the Razer DeathAdder V3 Good for Competitive Gaming?

The ergonomic shape, low weight, and 30K-DPI optical sensor are the kind of specs competitive and FPS players commonly prioritize, and the DeathAdder shape has a long track record in esports settings. Whether it's the right fit for you depends significantly on hand size and grip style (palm, claw, or fingertip) — those are worth testing in person or checking against Razer's own hand-size guidance before buying, since ergonomic mice that suit one grip style well can feel wrong for another.
