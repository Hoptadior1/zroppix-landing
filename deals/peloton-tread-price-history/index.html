Why a Repeated Price Is a Stronger Signal Than a Single Sale

The Cross Training Tread has hit $2,695 at three independently reported sale events across 2026 — Peloton's own Winter Savings Event, Amazon's Big Spring Sale, and Amazon Prime Day. That's a meaningfully different, more reliable signal than a single flash-sale price: it suggests $2,695 functions as something close to a recurring "true price" for this treadmill, rather than a one-time promotional low that may not return.

What this doesn't tell us is whether $2,695 is the absolute lowest this treadmill has ever sold for — we don't have a confirmed data point lower than that. If you're chasing the deepest possible discount and are willing to wait indefinitely with no guarantee, that's a different strategy than buying at a confirmed, repeated 18%-off price today.

Is the Peloton Cross Training Tread Worth It?

At $2,695, the Cross Training Tread offers a 24" HD swivel touchscreen, auto-incline that follows instructor cues, and Peloton's full running, walking, and strength class library. The $49.99/month All-Access Membership is required for live and on-demand content and should be factored into the total cost — that's roughly $600/year on top of the hardware price. For a household where more than one person runs regularly, the combined cost compares favorably to typical gym membership fees over a couple of years.

See our companion page on the original Peloton Bike's price history if you're deciding between cycling and running equipment.

Tread vs. Tread+ — Is the Extra $3,400 Worth It?

The Peloton Tread+ lists at $6,695 — more than double the Cross Training Tread's $3,295 list price. It adds a larger screen and a higher-end motor and belt built for more intensive daily use. For the large majority of runners, walkers, and HIIT users, the base Tread's specs cover what people actually use. The Tread+ makes sense mainly for serious daily runners who want the premium build and are less price-sensitive.
