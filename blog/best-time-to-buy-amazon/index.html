Is there really a best time to buy on Amazon? The evidence points to yes — though not every commonly-repeated "rule" holds up equally well, and at least one popular rule doesn't apply to every category. Here's what independent research actually supports, sourced rather than assumed.

💡 Short answer: Tuesday is the most consistently cited cheapest day to shop online overall, based on independent third-party research — though clothing specifically follows a different pattern (see below). Prime Day 2026 (Prime Big Deal Days) has already passed — if you're reading this in July, prices have climbed back up, and the next confirmed discount windows are Amazon's October Big Deal Days and Black Friday in November.

Best Days of the Week to Buy

Amazon reprices products constantly, and the timing isn't random — but it's worth being precise about where the "Tuesday and Wednesday" claim actually comes from, since it's repeated everywhere without a source.

DayTypical PatternVerdict

SaturdayTypically highest overall — but the single best day specifically for clothingAvoid (except clothing)

Sourced from a 2025 SmartProxy study that tracked pricing across 40 retailers every four hours for over a month (finding Tuesday cheapest overall, Friday most expensive overall), and separately corroborated by e-commerce experts quoted in GOBankingRates coverage on Amazon-specific pricing patterns.

Why does this pattern exist? The leading explanation from the experts cited above is that online demand is lower at the start of the week, so retailers compete more aggressively for the shoppers who are browsing — and ease off as demand naturally rises toward the weekend, when people have more free time to shop.

💡 Important exception: SmartProxy ran a separate, category-specific study on clothing (a six-week analysis across major retailers) and found the opposite pattern for that category — clothing prices were lowest on Saturday, with early Saturday morning the cheapest window, plus good prices on Friday afternoon and early Monday. If you're specifically shopping for clothes, the general "Tuesday is best" rule doesn't apply — see the Clothing section below.

The Truth About Black Friday on Amazon

Black Friday carries a reputation as the single best day to buy anything. The more accurate picture: it's genuinely excellent for some categories and unremarkable for others.

Where Black Friday is genuinely strong:

  • Amazon's own devices (Echo, Kindle, Fire TV) — these consistently see some of their deepest discounts of the year
  • Large appliances
  • TVs from major brands

For other categories, it's worth checking a product's actual price history around Black Friday rather than assuming the "Was" price on a Black Friday badge reflects genuine savings — some listings raise prices in the weeks before the event specifically so the "discount" looks bigger than it is. This is a well-documented retail pattern across many sale events, not unique to Amazon.

Best Time of Day to Buy

The same SmartProxy study cited above found prices tend to be cheapest between 6am and noon, especially on Tuesdays specifically — a more precise finding than the vaguer "early morning" advice repeated on many sites without a source. GOBankingRates' consulted experts separately suggested shopping early in the morning or late at night as a general rule of thumb, which is broadly consistent with the SmartProxy window.

For most people, structuring your shopping around a 6am-noon window isn't practical. That's exactly what price alerts solve — a tool that checks the price around the clock so you don't have to time it manually.

Clothing and Fashion

Best windows: end-of-season clearance, typically January, July, and September, when retailers clear out seasonal inventory. For weekly timing specifically, clothing is the one category where the general "shop Tuesday" rule doesn't apply — SmartProxy's dedicated clothing study found Saturday (especially early morning) is actually the cheapest day for this category, with Friday afternoon and Sunday also reasonable. Fashion pricing is also unusually volatile day-to-day: SmartProxy's research lead noted clothing shows meaningfully more aggressive dynamic pricing than categories like electronics or beauty, so checking the specific item's price history matters even more here than elsewhere.

Home and Kitchen

Best windows: January-February (post-holiday) and August (back-to-school adjacent), when home goods often see genuine markdowns.

Sports and Outdoors

Best windows: buying gear out of season — summer equipment in September/October, winter sports gear in March/April — is a well-established general retail pattern that typically applies on Amazon too.

Books

Kindle e-books see frequent, small discounts throughout the year. Physical books more often see meaningful markdowns during Prime Day and Black Friday specifically.
