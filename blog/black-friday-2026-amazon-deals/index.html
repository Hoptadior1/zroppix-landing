Black Friday and Cyber Monday 2026: the dates

The dates are fixed by the calendar, and Amazon's own schedule is the part that moves:

  • Thanksgiving: Thursday, November 26, 2026.
  • Black Friday: Friday, November 27, 2026.
  • Cyber Monday: Monday, November 30, 2026.

Amazon has not been tied to the Friday itself in recent years. It has tended to start its Black Friday promotions earlier in November and run them for days or weeks, with the biggest push around the Friday and the weekend after. We have not seen an official 2026 schedule at the time of writing, so treat any exact "starts on" date you read elsewhere as unconfirmed until Amazon announces it. We will update this page once it does.

Two practical points follow from that. First, a deal appearing early is not a reason to panic-buy: if the price is already at its yearly low, it may not go much lower on the Friday, but if it isn't, waiting costs you nothing. Second, Cyber Monday is not always the end. Some products hit their best price there, others earlier, which is exactly why you need the history instead of the calendar.

Why "Black Friday deal" doesn't mean "good price"

Retail events create pressure, and pressure is where bad decisions happen. These are the traps to expect:

  • Inflated "was" prices. A product shown at "40% off" may be compared to a list price it rarely, or never, sold at. The discount is real arithmetic against an unreal number. We explain how to check this in our guide to Amazon sale badges.
  • Countdown timers and "only X left". They create urgency, not value. See why they work on your brain.
  • Models made for the sale. Some retailers sell variants that look like a popular model but have lower specs. Check the exact model number.
  • Bundles that hide the price. A bundle can look cheap while including accessories you don't need.
  • Prices that creep up before the event. A product can edge up in the weeks before a sale so the "discount" looks bigger. Price history shows this; the sale badge doesn't. See our post on price increases before Prime Day, which applies to any sale.

The 4-step check for any Amazon deal

This takes under a minute and works on any product, any time of year.

  1. Open the price history. Amazon now shows its own price history on many product pages, with 30, 90 and 365 day views, in the U.S., UK, Canada and India, according to Amazon's May 2026 announcement. Third-party tools such as Zroppix, Keepa and CamelCamelCamel show it as well. Use whichever you prefer, but always look.
  2. Find the lowest price in the last year. Not the last month. A 30-day chart can make a mediocre price look like a bargain.
  3. Compare today's price to that low. If it's at or near the low, it's a genuinely good price. If it's well above, ask yourself whether you would pay it on any other day.
  4. Check the listing itself. Right model and size, sold by Amazon or a seller you trust, and a clear return policy for holiday purchases.

The only thing this check can't do is tell you the future. A price at its yearly low can still drop again, and a price well above it can stay there. History tells you what is normal. It doesn't promise what comes next, and anyone who says otherwise is selling something.

Prep now: a 20-minute plan before the sale

The best Black Friday work happens in October and early November, when you are calm and the prices are not yet part of a frenzy.

  1. Write your short list. Three to ten products you would genuinely buy. If it isn't on the list, it isn't a deal, it's an impulse.
  2. Look up each one's history. Note its normal price and its lowest price in the past year.
  3. Set your "yes" price. Decide in advance what you will pay. For many shoppers that is "at or within a few percent of the yearly low".
  4. Turn on alerts. Zroppix's free plan can track up to three products with email alerts, and other tools have their own limits. Pick your top three if you use a free plan.
  5. Check the basics now. Is the model the one you want? Is there a newer version about to replace it? Do you have somewhere to return it?

Then wait. If the price hits your number, buy. If it doesn't, you've lost nothing.

Buy now or wait? A simple decision rule

Use the price history to put the current price into one of four buckets:

  • At or near the yearly low: a good price. If you want the product, buying is reasonable.
  • A little above the low: decent, not special. Buy if you need it now; otherwise set an alert.
  • Well above the low: wait unless you have a reason not to. Sale badges here are mostly marketing.
  • No history (new product): be careful. With no baseline you can't tell a deal from a launch price, so lean on reviews and compare the price with alternatives.

The same rule works for Cyber Monday, a random Tuesday in March, or Prime Day. That is the point: you're buying on the price, not the event.

Mistakes that cost shoppers the most

  • Buying the wrong variant. Size, storage and colour can change the price and the value. Check the selected option before you pay.
  • Ignoring the seller. A cheap listing from an unfamiliar third-party seller carries more risk than one sold by Amazon.
  • Skipping the return window. Amazon has extended returns for holiday purchases in past years, but check this year's policy and any category exceptions.
  • Chasing the last 2%. If a price is at its yearly low, waiting for a few dollars more can mean missing stock.
  • Buying outdated models without noticing. A great price on last year's model can still be a bad buy if a major upgrade has just arrived.
  • Letting gift lists drive urgency. Decide the budget first, then the gifts.

Frequently asked questions

When is Black Friday 2026?

Friday, November 27, 2026, the day after Thanksgiving (Thursday, November 26). Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30.

When will Amazon's Black Friday deals start?

Amazon has not been limited to the Friday in recent years and has started promotions earlier in November. We haven't seen an official 2026 schedule yet, so check Amazon's deals page and our updates.

How do I know if a Black Friday deal is real?

Open the product's price history and compare the current price with its lowest price in the past year. If it's at or near that low, it's a real deal. If it's well above, it's marketing.

Does Amazon show price history itself?

Yes. Amazon says price history is available on product pages with 30, 90 and 365 day views in the U.S., UK, Canada and India, with the 365-day view rolling out. Not every listing is eligible.

Is it better to buy early or wait for Black Friday?

It depends on the product's history, not the calendar. If the price is already at its yearly low, buying early is fine. If not, an alert costs you nothing.

What if a product has no price history?

Be careful. With no baseline you can't tell a deal from a launch price. Compare it with alternatives and read reviews.

Can any tool predict price drops?

No tool can know a future price. Tools can show what's normal, and some offer estimates, but treat predictions as guesses, not promises.

Are Cyber Monday deals better than Black Friday?

Sometimes. Some products hit their lowest on Black Friday, others on Cyber Monday or on an ordinary day. History is a better guide than the event.

How many products should I track?

Three to ten you'd really buy. Zroppix's free plan tracks up to three with email alerts, so choose your top picks.

Beat the hype, not the clock

Black Friday rewards preparation. Build your list, learn the normal prices, and buy only when the price is genuinely low.
