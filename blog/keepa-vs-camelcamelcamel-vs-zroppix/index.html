What Each Tool Actually Is

CamelCamelCamel

CamelCamelCamel has been around the longest and is the most recognizable name in this space. It's a website-first tool: you paste a product URL (or search from their site), and it shows a price-history line graph going back months or years. It's entirely free, ad-supported, and lets you set email price-drop alerts. There's no dedicated browser extension in the way Keepa has one — most people use a bookmarklet or browse the CamelCamelCamel site directly.

Keepa

Keepa is the deepest of the three. Its browser extension embeds a price-history chart directly on the Amazon product page, and it tracks more than just price — sales rank history, Buy Box price and seller changes, and stock/out-of-stock periods. The free tier covers casual use well. The paid tiers (roughly $19 to $60+/month depending on plan) unlock bulk product tracking, longer data retention, and API access — features aimed squarely at Amazon sellers and resellers, not typical shoppers.

Zroppix

Zroppix takes a different approach on purpose: instead of showing you a chart and asking you to interpret it, it reads the same underlying price history and gives you a direct BUY or WAIT verdict the moment you open a listing. It's free, has no paid tier, and doesn't require an account. The tradeoff is that it isn't built for sellers who need sales rank or Buy Box data — it's built for the specific moment of "should I buy this right now."

Which One Should You Actually Use?

The honest answer depends entirely on what you're doing with the tool:

If you're an Amazon seller or reseller

Keepa's paid tier is genuinely built for this job — sales rank trends and Buy Box history aren't things CamelCamelCamel or Zroppix are trying to replicate, and it's worth the subscription if that data drives real sourcing or repricing decisions for you.

If you like reading a chart yourself and want it free

CamelCamelCamel remains a solid, no-cost option. The tradeoff is that you're the one interpreting the graph — deciding whether "the line dipped last month" means now is actually a good time to buy.

If you just want to know: buy now or wait?

This is the specific gap Zroppix was built to close. Instead of a graph, you get a direct verdict based on where the current price sits relative to its own recent history — useful in the moment you're actually deciding whether to click "Buy Now," without needing to interpret anything.

None of these three tools can see a price before it happens — all of them, including Zroppix, work from historical data. If a listing has no price history yet (a brand-new product), none of these tools can give you a meaningful verdict.
