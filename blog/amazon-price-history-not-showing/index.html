What Amazon's price history feature is (and where it should appear)

According to Amazon's own announcement (published May 1, 2026), shoppers can click a price history link directly on a product detail page, right next to the price. It offers 30, 90 and 365 day views, and shoppers can also ask Amazon's shopping assistant, Alexa for Shopping (renamed from Rufus on May 13, 2026), questions such as "What's the price history?" or "Is this the lowest price recently?"

The key limits, straight from Amazon's own page:

  • Price history is available to customers in the U.S., UK, Canada and India.
  • The 365-day view is rolling out in the U.S., UK and India, with full availability "in the coming weeks" at the time of the announcement.
  • During Prime Day, Amazon said the feature worked on "eligible product detail pages", without defining which products qualify.

Everything else about why a link might be missing is unconfirmed by Amazon. The rest of this guide separates what Amazon has said from what is likely.

6 likely reasons the link isn't showing

  1. Your country or Amazon store isn't covered. Amazon names four markets. If your account or delivery address is elsewhere, the link may simply not be available to you. This is the most likely cause for shoppers in most of Africa, the Middle East, Latin America and much of Asia and Europe who shop on amazon.com.
  2. The product isn't eligible. Amazon says only "eligible" product pages show it and doesn't explain eligibility. Brand-new listings, products with little sales history and some third-party items are the likeliest to lack enough data, though that's our inference, not Amazon's statement.
  3. The 365-day view hasn't reached you yet. If you see 30 and 90 days but not a year, the longer view may still be rolling out in your region.
  4. You're looking in the wrong place. The link sits next to the price on the product page, so look near the price and check that you've selected a specific size or colour. On some layouts you may need to scroll or expand the price block.
  5. An outdated app or browser. This is basic troubleshooting rather than something Amazon has confirmed: update the Amazon app, refresh the page, or try a different browser.
  6. Something is changing the page. Some extensions, ad blockers or privacy tools rewrite pages. Try the listing in a private window with extensions off to rule this out.

The 2-minute fix list

  1. Check you're in a covered country (U.S., UK, Canada or India) and on the matching Amazon store.
  2. Select a specific variant (size, colour, storage) on the product page.
  3. Look right next to the price for the price history link.
  4. Update the Amazon app or try the desktop site, and reload.
  5. Try a private window with extensions disabled.
  6. Try another product. If other items show the link and this one doesn't, the product probably isn't eligible.
  7. Use a third-party tracker (below) if the link still isn't there.

What to do if a product has no history anywhere

A brand-new product may have no useful price history on any tool. In that case:

  • Compare with similar products. Their histories show typical pricing for the category.
  • Check other retailers. A price that is much lower elsewhere is a useful signal.
  • Read recent reviews and check the seller.
  • Set an alert and wait, if you can. A few weeks of history is better than none.
  • Don't treat a launch price as a deal. A "deal" needs a baseline.

Why checking price history is worth the trouble

Independent research suggests that big retail sale events often don't deliver the savings they advertise. In a 2021 study published by the UK consumer group Which?, researchers tracked 214 Black Friday deals from seven retailers, checking prices daily. They found that 183 of them (86%) were the same price or cheaper in the six months before Black Friday, and none was cheaper on Black Friday alone. It's an older, UK-based study, so treat it as a caution, not a prediction for this year, but it's a good reason to look before you buy. Our Black Friday 2026 guide shows how to run the check.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't I see price history on Amazon?

Amazon says price history is available in the U.S., UK, Canada and India, on eligible product pages. If you're elsewhere or the product isn't eligible, the link may not appear. It's also worth selecting a specific variant, reloading and trying another browser.

Is Amazon price history available in Kenya or other countries?

Amazon's announcement names only the U.S., UK, Canada and India. Shoppers elsewhere may not see it, but third-party trackers such as Zroppix, Keepa and CamelCamelCamel show history on Amazon pages.

Where is the price history link on Amazon?

Amazon says it is a link directly on the product detail page, right next to the price. You can also ask Amazon's shopping assistant, Alexa for Shopping, about a product's price history.

How far back does Amazon's price history go?

Amazon says it offers 30, 90 and 365 day views. The 365-day view was still rolling out in the U.S., UK and India at the time of its announcement.

Why does price history show for some products but not others?

Amazon says only eligible product pages show it and doesn't explain eligibility. New or low-volume listings are the likeliest to lack data, though that's our inference.

Does it work in the Amazon app?

Amazon's announcement describes the link on product detail pages and Alexa for Shopping in the Amazon Shopping app. If you can't find it in the app, try the desktop site, and update the app.

Is Amazon's price history accurate?

It's Amazon's own record of prices on its own site. We can't audit it, so for big purchases, cross-check with a second tool.

How can I see Amazon price history for free?

Use Amazon's built-in link where available, or a free tracker such as Zroppix, CamelCamelCamel, Keepa's free features or Honey.

What if no tool shows history for my product?

The product may be too new. Compare similar products, check other retailers, read reviews and set an alert.

Don't buy blind just because the link is missing

Try the fix list, then use a tracker. A minute checking price history is the cheapest way to avoid a bad deal.
