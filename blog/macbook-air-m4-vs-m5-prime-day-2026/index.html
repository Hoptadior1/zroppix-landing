For Students — The M4 Wins Clearly

The performance difference between M4 and M5 is measurable in benchmarks and invisible in everyday student use. Writing essays, running Zoom classes, using Google Docs, browsing research, streaming lectures, running Xcode for CS students, running Office apps, editing photos lightly — the M4 and M5 perform identically in all of these tasks.

The M5's advantage — a faster GPU and larger Neural Engine — matters for three specific workloads: exporting 4K video, running local AI/ML models, and sustained GPU compute tasks. These are workloads that professional video editors and ML engineers perform. They are not workloads that most students perform regularly.

The M4 at $749-799 Prime Day versus the M5 at $849-879 Prime Day is a $100 difference for the same real-world student experience. That $100 buys a year of Adobe Creative Cloud, a quality pair of headphones, or several textbooks — much better value than a performance upgrade you will not notice in daily use.

Who Should Buy the M5 Instead?

The MacBook Air M5 is not the wrong product — it is the wrong product at Prime Day pricing compared to the M4 deal available simultaneously. There are buyers for whom the M5 makes more sense at Prime Day.

Buy the M5 if you: export 4K or ProRes video regularly, run local Large Language Models (LLMs), use Final Cut Pro professionally, work in machine learning or data science with heavy compute requirements, or plan to keep the laptop for 6+ years and want to stay current-generation for longer.

For everyone else — and that includes the overwhelming majority of students — the M4 at $749-799 Prime Day is the rational purchase. The $100 saved versus the M5 at Prime Day pricing has far more practical value than the performance benchmark improvement you will never notice in word processing, web browsing, or coding.
