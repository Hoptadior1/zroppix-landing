Why people are looking for a Honey alternative

Honey is a free browser extension, owned by PayPal, that finds coupon codes and also offers price history and price-drop alerts. In December 2024, a YouTuber known as MegaLag published an investigation alleging that Honey changed affiliate tracking at checkout so that Honey, rather than the creator whose link the shopper had clicked, earned the commission. PayPal disputes the allegations and said at the time that "Honey follows industry rules and practices, including last-click attribution" (statement to The Verge, December 23, 2024).

The fallout is documented. Honey's Chrome user count fell from more than 20 million to about 14 million by July 2025 (PPC Land, from Chrome Web Store data), and affiliate networks removed it in January 2026. The timeline below sets out what is sourced and what is only alleged.

What was alleged, in plain terms

Affiliate links work on "last-click attribution": whichever tracked link you clicked most recently gets the commission when you buy. The allegation was that when a shopper arrived through a creator's link and then used Honey at checkout, Honey's own tracking became the last click and took the commission, including in cases where Honey found no discount. PayPal's position is that this is standard industry practice. Courts have not found Honey liable; see the June 2026 ruling in the timeline, which only allowed the case to proceed.

A later MegaLag video (December 2025) made further claims, summarised on Wikipedia, about Honey's handling of coupon codes and data. We list them in the timeline as allegations, and we don't treat them as proven.

What Honey still does well

It is fair to say what Honey does. Its own Amazon page lists Amazon Price History (recent price changes in one click), price drop alerts through a Droplist, and a coupon search across 30,000+ sites. So "Honey has no price history" would be wrong. If you shop across many stores and want coupons, Honey does something Zroppix does not.

Where it is weaker for Amazon shoppers is the verdict: Honey shows data, and you decide. And on Amazon, coupon codes are less common than on smaller retailers, so price history is usually the more useful half.

Why price history beats coupons on Amazon

  • It works on every listing. A coupon exists for a small share of products. History exists for nearly all.
  • It catches fake discounts. A 20% off badge on a price that was inflated last week is not a deal. See our guide to sale badges.
  • It tells you when to wait. A coupon helps at checkout. History helps before you add to cart.

Zroppix: what it is and what it isn't

Zroppix is a Chrome and Brave extension that works on Amazon. It reads a product's recent price history and gives a plain BUY or WAIT call, a savings estimate and a 90-day history right on the listing. The free plan includes email price alerts for up to three products. Paid plans add AI-based drop predictions, which are estimates, not promises. By design, Zroppix does not change affiliate links or tracking cookies.

Limits, honestly: it works on Amazon only, its history window is 90 days, it can't judge a brand-new product with no history, and it doesn't find coupon codes. For a big purchase, cross-check with a second tool (see our price tracker comparison).

Frequently asked questions

What happened with the Honey affiliate controversy?

In December 2024, YouTuber MegaLag alleged that Honey replaced creators' affiliate tracking with its own at checkout. PayPal said Honey follows industry rules including last-click attribution. Class actions followed, and Rakuten Advertising terminated Honey on January 12, 2026.

Is Honey still working?

The extension still exists and still offers coupons, Amazon price history and Droplist alerts per its own page. Its Chrome user count fell from more than 20 million to about 14 million by July 2025 (PPC Land).

Did Rakuten really drop Honey?

Yes. Hello Partner reported on January 12, 2026 that Rakuten Advertising terminated Honey from its network effective immediately, without stating a reason. impact.com suspended Honey on January 17, 2026 (PPC Land).

Has Honey been found guilty of anything?

No. On June 22, 2026 a federal judge denied PayPal's renewed motion to dismiss, so the case continues. PPC Land notes that this is not a finding of liability.

Does Honey have price history?

Yes. Honey's Amazon page lists Amazon Price History and price drop alerts through a Droplist.

What is the best Honey alternative for Amazon?

If you want price history and a plain BUY or WAIT call, try Zroppix. For simple free charts, try CamelCamelCamel. For deep data, try Keepa. For coupons across many stores, Honey does that.

Does Zroppix change affiliate links?

No. By design, Zroppix doesn't change affiliate links or tracking cookies.

Is Zroppix free?

The core features are free: BUY or WAIT calls, a 90-day history and email alerts for up to three products. Paid plans add predictions and more.

Should I uninstall Honey?

That's your call. If you only used it on Amazon, a price-history tool covers more. If you use its coupons on other stores, you can keep both.
