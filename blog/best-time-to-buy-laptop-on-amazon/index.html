Best Time To Buy a Laptop on Amazon in 2026

Buying a laptop at the wrong time of year can mean paying full price for a machine that's discounted 20%+ a few weeks later. Here's when laptop discounts actually happen — based on retail sales data, not guesswork — and how to check whether a specific listing is a genuine deal.

✍️ Zroppix Team ⏱ 9 min read 🔄 Updated July 21, 2026

A laptop is not a casual purchase. At $500, $800, $1,200 or more — the timing of your purchase matters. The general pattern across retail sales data is consistent: laptops discount most during two windows each year, and buying outside them usually means paying closer to full price.

⚡ Quick Answer

The two most reliable laptop discount windows are Back to School (late July-August), typically 8-20% off depending on the category, and Black Friday/Cyber Monday (late November), typically 20-35% off — especially strong on gaming laptops. Premium ultrabooks and MacBooks generally see smaller percentage discounts than budget and mid-range models due to stricter manufacturer pricing policies.

Why Amazon can beat the Apple Store on MacBooks

Apple enforces strict minimum advertised pricing rules, and the Apple Store itself almost never discounts MacBooks directly. Authorized resellers like Amazon periodically offer meaningful markdowns on MacBook Air and MacBook Pro models during sale events like Black Friday — discounts Apple rarely if ever matches at its own store. If a MacBook discount exists at all, it's more likely to show up through an authorized reseller than direct from Apple.

When to buy a gaming laptop

Gaming laptops from brands like ASUS ROG, Razer, MSI, and Alienware tend to follow the deepest discount pattern of any laptop category — 20-35% off is common during Black Friday and Cyber Monday specifically, since new GPU generations typically launch in the fall, pushing current-generation models toward clearance pricing. For a brand-new flagship gaming laptop released close to Black Friday, discounts tend to be weaker — waiting a few months after release often works better than expecting a launch-week markdown.

Laptop Buying Mistakes That Cost Real Money

Mistake 1 — Buying right before a known sale window

If Back to School or Black Friday is a few weeks away, buying immediately beforehand is the single easiest way to overpay. If you don't need the laptop urgently, checking how close you are to one of the two major windows before buying is worth the wait in most cases.

Mistake 2 — Buying before checking if a new model is coming

Before buying any laptop, spend a minute searching "[laptop name] release date" or checking the manufacturer's typical annual refresh cycle. If a new version is due in the next couple of months, waiting usually means either the new model at launch price or the current model at a meaningful clearance discount — both outcomes beat buying the outgoing model at full price right before it's replaced.

Mistake 3 — Trusting sale badges without checking price history

Amazon's "% off" badges are calculated against a reference price that isn't always the item's real recent selling price. A badge showing a discount doesn't guarantee the current price is actually below what the item has sold for recently. Checking real price history — not just the badge — is the only reliable way to confirm a genuine deal.
