If you missed June's Prime Day, the good news is Amazon runs sale events almost every month — you just have to know when the real ones are. Labor Day blends end-of-summer clearance with back-to-school leftovers, and it's genuinely underrated: less competition for attention than Prime Day, but often comparable deal quality on laptops, tablets, and smart home gear. Prime Big Deal Days in October is Amazon's second Prime-exclusive event of the year and typically the unofficial start of holiday shopping — it generated an estimated $11 billion in U.S. sales in its 2025 edition.

How to Actually Know a Deal Is Real When One Hits

The trap with any of these events is the same one we've covered before: a "40% off" banner means nothing if the "before" price was inflated a few weeks earlier. The fix is knowing the real, independently tracked floor a product has hit in past sale cycles — so when Labor Day or Big Deal Days rolls around, you can compare the live price against actual history, not against Amazon's own comparison number.
