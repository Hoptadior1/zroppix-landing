The Coanda Effect — What It Actually Is and Whether It Matters

Dyson's core innovation in the Airwrap is Coanda airflow — a physics principle where fluid (in this case air) naturally follows a curved surface. Dyson's engineering directs high-speed air around the barrel in a way that attracts hair toward it and wraps it automatically. This is the feature that made the Airwrap viral: you hold the barrel near your hair and it reaches for the barrel, wraps itself, and curls without manual technique.

The Shark FlexStyle's auto-wrap curler is a direct response to this. Rather than true Coanda physics, FlexStyle uses a directed airflow design combined with a specific barrel shape to encourage hair to wrap. It requires slightly more conscious technique — you need to direct the hair toward the barrel rather than just hold it nearby. The result is functionally similar but the process feels slightly more manual.

For most users, this distinction disappears after 3-5 uses. The technique becomes second nature. But for complete beginners who have never used a styler before, the Dyson's more automatic behaviour does genuinely reduce the learning curve. If you are buying your first ever multi-styler and styling technique is not something you have experience with, the Airwrap's automatic wrap is the easier entry point.

Heat Damage — Which Tool Is Safer for Your Hair?

Both the Shark FlexStyle and Dyson Airwrap are marketed as lower-heat alternatives to traditional curling irons and straighteners. Traditional curling irons heat to 300-450°F — temperatures that cause measurable cuticle damage with repeated use. Both multi-stylers operate at significantly lower temperatures by using airflow rather than direct contact heat as the primary styling mechanism.

The Dyson Airwrap measures temperature 40 times per second and regulates heat to stay below the threshold where heat damage begins (around 150°C / 302°F). The Shark FlexStyle uses a similar intelligent heat control system. Both are meaningfully safer than traditional heat styling tools.

For damaged, colour-treated, or fine hair that is particularly heat-sensitive, both tools are a significant improvement over curling wands and straighteners. The Dyson's temperature regulation is slightly more sophisticated, which gives it a marginal edge for very fine or heavily processed hair. For normal to thick hair, both tools deliver comparable heat safety.
