Bought Something on Amazon Then Price Dropped? Here Is Exactly What To Do

Amazon eliminated its official price adjustment policy in 2016 — but you still have 4 real options if the price drops after you buy. Here's exactly what to do based on your situation, and how to check before you buy so this happens less often.

✍️ Zroppix Team ⏱ 10 min read 🔄 Updated July 26, 2026

You bought something on Amazon. You felt fine about the price. Then the price dropped — sometimes within hours. The frustration is real, and you weren't careless: Amazon's pricing moves constantly, for reasons that have nothing to do with your specific purchase. Here's what to actually do about it, and what's realistic to expect from each option.

⚡ Quick Answer

If the order hasn't shipped — cancel and reorder immediately. If it has shipped, contact Amazon customer service and politely ask for a courtesy credit — this is discretionary and not guaranteed, but costs nothing to try. Within your return window, return and repurchase at the lower price. If your credit card has a price-protection benefit, check whether it applies. All 4 methods explained in detail below.

Amazon eliminated its formal price-adjustment policy in 2016 and does not automatically refund the difference when prices drop after purchase. Customer service can issue discretionary courtesy credits, but this isn't a published policy — treat it as a possibility worth trying, not a guarantee.

The 4 Real Methods — Ranked by Reliability

Method 1 ✅ Most Reliable — When Available

Cancel and reorder before it ships

If your order hasn't shipped yet, this is the cleanest option. Cancel and immediately place a new order at the lower price. Amazon processes cancellations instantly for unshipped orders, and your refund returns to your original payment method within a few business days.

Steps:
  1. Go to Your Orders and find the order
  2. Click "Cancel Items" → select the items → confirm
  3. Wait for the cancellation confirmation email
  4. Reorder the same product at the new lower price
Method 2 🟡 Discretionary — Worth Trying, Not Guaranteed

Ask customer service for a courtesy credit

Amazon has no published price-adjustment policy, but customer service agents do have discretion to issue one-time courtesy credits — particularly for smaller drops requested soon after delivery. Reported success is inconsistent: some shoppers get a credit, many get declined, and success appears to drop sharply during major promotional periods like Prime Day. It costs nothing to ask, so it's worth trying before assuming you'll need to return the item.

One genuinely official exception worth knowing: Amazon's Pre-order Price Guarantee is a real, documented policy — if you pre-order an eligible item sold directly by Amazon and the price drops before release, you're charged the lowest price. That's different from this general courtesy-credit situation, which is discretionary.

Steps:
  1. Go to Help → Contact Us → select your order → Live Chat
  2. State the order date, price paid, and the current lower price with a link
  3. Ask politely whether any credit is possible — don't cite a policy that doesn't exist
  4. If declined, thank them and move to the return-and-repurchase method instead
Method 3 ✅ Reliable Fallback Within the Return Window

Return and repurchase at the lower price

Amazon's standard return window is 30 days from delivery for most items. If the price dropped meaningfully and you're within that window, returning the item and immediately repurchasing at the lower price is the one option that's genuinely reliable, regardless of what customer service decides. Weigh the return-shipping hassle (or cost, if not free) against the size of the price difference before doing this for small drops.

Steps:
  1. Start the return from Your Orders
  2. Choose the lowest-friction free return option available for your order (drop-off locations vary)
  3. Repurchase the same product at the lower price
  4. Drop off the return; the original refund typically processes within a few business days of confirmation
Method 4 Depends Entirely On Your Specific Card

Credit card price protection — if your card still has it

Some credit cards offer price protection: an automatic refund of the difference if an item you bought drops in price within a set window, often 60 days. This benefit has been significantly scaled back industry-wide — Chase discontinued it on consumer cards in 2018, and several other issuers have followed. Some Visa Signature/Infinite cards (including select Wells Fargo and U.S. Bank cards) still offer a Visa-administered version, typically capped around $250 per claim. Confirm directly with your card's current benefits guide before relying on this — don't assume based on what a card offered in the past.

Why the Price Dropped Right After You Bought

The actual mechanism

It's the repricing algorithm, not you specifically

Amazon's repricing system changes prices at massive scale — industry estimates put it around 2.5 million price changes a day across Amazon's own retail catalog, with third-party marketplace sellers repricing even more frequently. This is driven by competitor pricing, Buy Box competition among sellers, current demand, and inventory levels.

Amazon has publicly and repeatedly stated it does not price based on an individual shopper's profile, demographics, or browsing history — a position it took explicitly after a 2000 controversy involving exactly this accusation. So a price drop right after your purchase is best explained by ordinary algorithmic timing, not anything specific to you or your account.

That's actually useful framing: it means the fix isn't "browse less before buying" — it's checking a listing's real price history before you commit, since that tells you whether you're buying near a low point or not, independent of any theory about being personally tracked.
