How Amazon Creates Fake Prime Day Deals

Understanding the mechanism protects you from falling for it next time. Here is exactly how manufactured Prime Day deals work:

Step 1 — Price inflation before Prime Day

In the 4-8 weeks before Prime Day, Amazon and third-party sellers raise prices on products they plan to "discount." The algorithm begins tracking this elevated price as the new reference point.

Step 2 — The crossed-out "was" price

The "was" price on Amazon — the crossed-out higher number — is called the reference price. Amazon's guidelines say this should be a recent selling price. In practice, sellers set the reference price at the inflated pre-Prime Day price they have been charging for the past 4-8 weeks, even though that was not the normal price before that period.

Step 3 — The Prime Day "deal"

When Prime Day arrives the price drops back to what it was before the inflation period. Amazon displays the discount percentage against the inflated reference price — making a 0% actual saving look like a 25-40% discount.

A product that sold for $50 in March, was raised to $70 in April-May, and then "discounted" to $60 during Prime Day showed as "14% off" — but was actually 20% more than shoppers would have paid 3 months earlier.

🚨 Amazon changed its reference pricing guidelines in 2024 following regulatory scrutiny, but enforcement continues to vary significantly by seller and category. This year's data shows manufactured discounts remain widespread — particularly from third-party sellers in clothing, beauty, and unknown-brand electronics.
