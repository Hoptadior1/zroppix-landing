Amazon Charges Different People Different Prices — Here Is the Proof (2026)

📅 May 15, 2026 ⏱ 7 min read ✍️ Zroppix Team 🔄 Updated May 2026

You and your friend open the same Amazon product at the same time. You see $89.99. They see $79.99. Same product. Same listing. Same moment. Different price.

This is not a bug. It is not a glitch. It is Amazon's pricing algorithm working exactly as designed. And it is happening to millions of shoppers every single day.

🚨 Amazon makes 2.5 million price changes per day. Its algorithm studies your browsing history, device type, location, Prime membership, and purchase patterns — then charges you the maximum it calculates you will pay. Different users see different prices for the same product.

The 6 Factors Amazon Uses To Set Your Personal Price

1

Your Browsing History

If you have visited a product page multiple times Amazon knows you want it. High purchase intent = higher price. Users who view a product once often see lower prices than users who have viewed it repeatedly.

2

Your Device Type

Studies have found price differences between iPhone users, Android users, and desktop users shopping for the same product. Apple device users have sometimes been shown higher prices based on inferred income levels.

3

Your Location

Amazon uses geographic pricing. The same product can cost different amounts depending on your ZIP code or region. Areas with higher average incomes sometimes see higher prices for identical products.

4

Your Prime Membership Status

Prime members and non-Prime users can see different prices. The algorithm knows Prime members are more likely to purchase and more loyal to Amazon — which can result in different pricing strategies.

5

Time of Day

Amazon's algorithm adjusts prices throughout the day based on demand signals. Prices can be different at 9am versus 9pm for the same product. Shopping at off-peak times can sometimes yield lower prices.

6

Your Purchase History

Amazon knows exactly what you have bought before, what you paid, and what categories you shop in. This data informs its pricing model — users with demonstrated willingness to pay premium prices can be shown higher prices.
