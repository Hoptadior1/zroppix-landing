Why Chart Interpretation Is the Wrong Model

📊

Charts require expertise most shoppers do not have

Accurately reading a price history chart requires understanding statistical averages, recognizing seasonal patterns, identifying trend lines, and contextualizing anomalies. Most shoppers do not have this expertise. They make rough visual estimates that are frequently wrong.

⏱️

High friction means people skip the check

Any process that takes more than 10 seconds gets skipped on purchases that feel obviously fine. Sale badges, countdown timers, and "was" prices make most Amazon purchases feel obviously fine. These are exactly the purchases most likely to be overpriced. High-friction checks are skipped precisely when they are most needed.

🧠

Visual estimates introduce confirmation bias

When you want to buy something, your visual estimate of a price history chart trends toward "the current price looks okay." Confirmation bias is well-documented in purchasing decisions. A pre-calculated verdict based on objective data removes this bias entirely.

💰

Wrong interpretations cost real money

A shopper who misreads a CamelCamelCamel chart and buys at an above-average price loses $20-50 per purchase. At the documented rate of 54% of Amazon products being above their average at any given moment — this happens frequently. A wrong verdict costs money every time.
