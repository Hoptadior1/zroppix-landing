How Amazon Manipulates Prime Day Prices

Understanding this pattern helps you evaluate every Prime Day deal you see — not just the five products above.

Amazon's "Prime Day deal" price is always shown against a reference price — the "was $X" figure that makes the discount look impressive. What most shoppers do not know is that Amazon allows sellers to raise their reference prices in the weeks before Prime Day, so the discount percentage appears larger on the day. A product that genuinely sells for $45 year-round can list as "was $79.99" on Prime Day with a "43% off" tag — and it never actually sold for $79.99 at any meaningful volume.

This is why checking 90-day price history before buying any Prime Day deal is not optional — it is the only way to know if you are actually getting a deal. Zroppix's price history shows what the product actually sold for over the past 90 days, not what the seller claims it was worth.

The "Lightning Deal" countdown timer on Prime Day creates artificial urgency. Research shows that most Lightning Deals reset or restock multiple times during Prime Day. Never buy something just because a timer says 47 minutes remaining. Check the price history first.
