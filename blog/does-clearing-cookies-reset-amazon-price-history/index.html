Does Clearing Cookies Reset Your Amazon Price History?

It's one of the most repeated pieces of online shopping advice — clear your cookies and the price drops. Here's what Amazon has actually said, where the myth came from, and what's really happening when it seems to work.

✍️ Zroppix Team ⏱ 7 min read 🔄 Updated July 26, 2026

You've probably seen the advice: clear your cookies, or shop in incognito, and Amazon will show you a lower price. It's repeated constantly across shopping forums and "money-saving hacks" lists. So does it actually work — and if so, why?

⚡ Quick Answer

Amazon has publicly stated it does not price based on individual customer profiles, following a real controversy in 2000 where it was accused of exactly that. When people genuinely do see a price change after clearing cookies, the far more likely explanation is coincidental timing: Amazon's repricing algorithm changes millions of prices a day for reasons that have nothing to do with any single shopper's cookies.

Where the Myth Actually Comes From

2000
Amazon faced public backlash after some customers noticed the same DVD priced differently depending on their cookie/account state — appearing to charge existing customers more than new visitors. Amazon's founder publicly stated the company had never tested pricing based on customer demographics, called it a random price experiment, and refunded shoppers who'd paid the higher price.
2012 onward
Researchers documented real cases of other retailers (not Amazon specifically) adjusting prices based on a shopper's location or browsing device — for example, one study found some hotel booking prices varied by the country associated with a visitor's IP address.
Today
The 2000 incident is still the most-cited "proof" that cookie-clearing works on Amazon specifically — but it's a 25-year-old anecdote, not evidence of an ongoing practice Amazon has said it doesn't do.

So Why Do People Still Report It "Working"?

The most likely explanation isn't that clearing cookies caused the price to change — it's that the price was going to change anyway. Given Amazon reprices at a scale of roughly 2.5 million changes a day across its retail catalog, and third-party marketplace listings even more often, checking a price, clearing your cookies, and checking again later is very likely to land on two different price snapshots — regardless of the cookie step in between.

🔍 The Actual Mechanism
Timing coincidence, not cause and effect
The repricing algorithm runs continuously in the background. If you happen to check again after clearing cookies and the price is lower, it's very likely the algorithm moved the price for its own reasons in that window — not your browser state.

Incognito mode does stop personalized ad targeting and session-based tracking — it just doesn't change the underlying price Amazon shows for a listing, since that price isn't being generated per-browser-session in the first place.

Cookies and Amazon prices — FAQ
Does clearing cookies actually lower Amazon prices?+
Amazon has publicly stated it does not price based on individual customer profiles, so clearing cookies shouldn't causally change a price. When people do see a drop after clearing cookies, it's most likely coincidental timing with Amazon's constant repricing algorithm.
Did Amazon ever get caught personalizing prices?+
Yes, in 2000 Amazon faced controversy when some customers noticed different DVD prices after clearing cookies. Amazon said it was a random price test unrelated to demographics, apologized, and refunded affected customers.
Does using incognito mode change Amazon prices?+
Incognito mode prevents session-based tracking and personalized ads, but it doesn't change the underlying price Amazon shows, which is driven by its repricing algorithm rather than your individual browser session.
What actually causes an Amazon price to look different later?+
Amazon's automated repricing system changes prices constantly based on competitor pricing, demand, and inventory — independent of any single shopper's cookies or browsing history.
