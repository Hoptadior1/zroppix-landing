Why You Need a Price History Extension

Amazon is not always cheap. The platform uses dynamic pricing — meaning prices change constantly based on demand, competition, and time of year. A deal badge on a product does not mean the price is actually low. It might be the highest it has been in months.

A price history Chrome extension solves this by showing you what a product has actually cost over time — so you can tell instantly whether today's price is a real deal or a trap.

2. Keepa

Keepa is the most widely used Amazon price tracker on the market with over 4 million Chrome users and a 4.7 out of 5 star rating. It tracks over 5 billion Amazon products across 11 international marketplaces and displays detailed price history charts directly on the Amazon product page.

The free version gives you access to price history charts. The paid tier unlocks advanced alerts via email, browser notifications, and Telegram, plus seller tracking and API access. Keepa is powerful but the charts can feel overwhelming if you just want a simple answer.

  • Price history — Years of data on billions of products
  • AI verdict — No — you read the chart yourself
  • Price alerts — Yes, paid tier required for full alerts
  • Free plan — Basic charts only
  • Premium — Required for full features
Best for: Power users and Amazon resellers who want deep historical data
Best Free Option

3. CamelCamelCamel (The Camelizer)

CamelCamelCamel has been tracking Amazon prices since 2008 making it the original price history tool. The Camelizer Chrome extension overlays price history charts on Amazon product pages and lets you set email price alerts for free with no account required.

It covers Amazon in the US, UK, Canada, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Australia, and Japan. The main downside is that some users report price alert delays and the interface feels dated compared to newer tools. It currently has around 190,000 Chrome users and a 4.3 out of 5 star rating.

  • Price history — Years of data, completely free
  • AI verdict — No — charts only
  • Price alerts — Yes, free email alerts
  • Free plan — Fully free, no account needed
Best for: Casual shoppers who want simple free price checks
Best for Coupons

4. Honey (PayPal Honey)

Honey is best known for automatically finding and applying coupon codes at checkout across 30,000 plus sites. Its Droplist feature also lets you set price drop alerts on Amazon, Walmart, Target, and eBay. With over 17 million Chrome users it is one of the most installed shopping extensions in existence.

The price history features are less detailed than Keepa or CamelCamelCamel — you get basic trend graphs rather than deep historical data. If your main goal is finding coupon codes and cashback, Honey is great. For serious price history research, it falls short.

  • Price history — Basic graphs only
  • AI verdict — No
  • Coupon codes — Automatic at checkout
  • Price alerts — Yes, via Droplist
  • Free plan — Completely free
Best for: Shoppers who want automatic coupon codes plus basic alerts
Lightweight Pick

5. PriceBlink

PriceBlink takes a different approach. Instead of showing price history, it pops up a notification bar when you are on a product page showing whether the same item is available cheaper somewhere else — Walmart, Target, Best Buy, and other retailers. It has over 100,000 Chrome downloads and a 4.5 star rating.

There is no price history chart and no future price alerts. It is purely a cross-retailer price comparison tool. Simple, fast, and does one thing well. No account required.

  • Price history — No
  • AI verdict — No
  • Cross-retailer comparison — Yes
  • Price alerts — No
  • Free plan — Completely free

How To Get The Most From Any Price History Extension

1

Always check before you buy

Make it a habit. Before clicking Add to Cart on anything over $20, open your price tracker. It takes five seconds and can save you serious money.

2

Compare current price to the average — not the all-time low

The all-time low might have been a one-day flash sale years ago. A more realistic target is 10-15% below the 90-day average price.

3

Set a price alert and walk away

If the price is currently high, set an alert at your target price and forget about it. Let the technology do the work instead of checking manually every day.

4

Watch for fake deal inflation

Amazon sometimes raises a price before a sale event then marks it down to look like a discount. Price history exposes this instantly — always check the chart before trusting a sale badge.
